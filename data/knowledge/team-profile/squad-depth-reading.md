## What squad depth is, and what it is not

Depth is not the number of names on the bench. It is the number of *different jobs* the squad can do credibly across a season, without diluting the best eleven beyond what the fixture asks. A squad can hold many players of similar profile and no depth — lose one centre-back to suspension and find no two who cover the role at the same level without a step down in quality. Depth reading is arithmetic on roles, not on shirts.

## Count lines the way a selection meeting does

Ask three questions of any roster, in order. How many players can lead each of the four lines — defence, midfield, attack, goalkeeping — through a full season without anyone noticing the difference? How many can honestly cover each of the others if required? How many can start anywhere at all? A club whose defensive pool shares one age curve and one playing style has not bought depth; it has bought repetition. A club with one genuine problem-solver per line and one emergency cover each usually has more selection room than a club with a deeper but identical-looking list.

## The overlap you can never select

Talent and depth are not the same thing. A dressing room can hold enormous talent and tiny depth if it overlaps completely — ten playmakers and no carrier to break pressure, no six to win the ball and restart. The honest selection test: for the formation the manager actually wants to play, who would be the eleven under the worst suspension sequence and the worst recovery week? If the answer is the same eleven shifted one shirt along, the squad has overlap where it should have spread. The jobs the squad needs filled are not "better versions of the same man"; they are the jobs nobody on the pitch can currently leave to carry.

## Why September is a diagnostic too

The cleanest test a squad's depth takes is the international week, when the pool simply shrinks. The team that stayed identical in shape after two internationals away is a different squad from the one that stretched. The question worth asking at any point in the season is not "is our best eleven the best eleven we have seen?" but "if two of it were unavailable, whose name would the manager put on the paper?" Depth lives or dies in that answer, and every player with a name for it gets bought cheaper than the undisputed starter would cost at the same age.

## Reading depth is not judging players

None of this is an instruction to discard talent that burns to play the same role as its rival. It is a worksheet for the staff: understand what the squad can do rotated, rotated, rotated, and what it cannot do rotated once — then spend the off-season like a squad being told its true answer. A team built around its eleven best names is honest every Saturday only if the twelve, thirteenth and fourteenth are honest versions of themselves too.
