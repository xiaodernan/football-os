## The short answer

The 2013–14 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Estádio da Luz in Lisbon, Portugal, on 24 May 2014 at 19:45 local time under partly cloudy 17 °C weather, in front of 60,976 spectators. Real Madrid beat Atlético Madrid 4–1 after extra time to claim their tenth European Cup, La Décima, twelve years after their ninth (Madrid 2–1 Leverkusen, 2002). Goalscorers: Atlético's Diego Godín 36' (Juanfran's header from a clearance fell nicely for Godín, who looped his finish over Iker Casillas); Sergio Ramos 93' (Luka Modrić's corner made room; Ramos delivered a diving header into the left side of the net to equalise in stoppage time of the regulation 90); Gareth Bale 110' (Ángel Di María dribbled past three Atlético defenders, shot; Thibaut Courtois blocked the Argentine's shot, the rebound fell to Bale to head from two yards); Marcelo 118' (Ronaldo squared the ball to Marcelo who finished with his left foot from inside the penalty area); Cristiano Ronaldo 120' (a stoppage-time penalty — given when Gabi tripped Ronaldo — into the left corner; his 17th goal of the tournament). Referee Björn Kuipers (Netherlands); Player of the match Ángel Di María (Fans' Player of the match: Sergio Ramos).

## How the match was played

Atlético arrived at Lisbon having won La Liga a week earlier — their first top-flight title since 1996 — on a defensive compactness that had stifled Madrid all season in both La Liga fixtures (1–0 win for Atlético, 2–2 draw in Madrid) and at the Copa del Rey semi-final (Madrid won 3–0 at home and 2–0 away). Real Madrid instead arrived punctuating the season with a 5–0 aggregate defeat of Bayern Munich in the semi-finals (1–0 at home; 4–0 at the Allianz Arena).

Diego Costa limped off inside the opening eight minutes on a hamstring issue already quietly on his file; Atlético continued without him. Gareth Bale had Real's best chance just after the half-hour mark — only a few minutes later Atlético punished this miss. Around the 36th minute Atlético received a corner; the routine sent Juanfran's header back into the penalty area onto Godín, who caught Casillas off his line with a looping header to make it 1–0. Then the decisive ninety-plus: Ramos answered in the third minute of stoppage time in regulation — a Modrić corner — with a diving header that reached the left side of the net. The match then leapt into extra time with no substitutions left for either team — Atlético's extremely narrow squad really was burning gas.

## What the route told us

Real Madrid route: Group B on 16 points ahead of Galatasaray (7), Juventus drawn twice, Copenhagen both games won; knockouts — Schalke fell 9–2 on aggregate (6–1 away, then 3–1 at the Bernabéu), Borussia Dortmund fell 3–2 (3–0 at home; 0–2 away), Bayern Munich fell 5–0 (1–0 at home; 4–0 at the Allianz Arena). Atlético route: Group G winners on 16 points unbeaten, with wins both over Zenit (3–1 at home; 1–1 away) and 2–1 wins at Porto and 2–0 against Porto at home, plus two wins over Austria Wien; knockouts — AC Milan fell 5–1 (1–0 away, 4–1 home in extra time), Barcelona fell 2–1 (1–1 at Camp Nou, 1–0 at the Calderón), Chelsea fell 3–1 (0–0 at the Calderón, 3–1 at Stamford Bridge).

## What the final meant

This was the first ever final to feature two Spanish clubs from the same city; it was also the second all-Spanish final (after 2000, Real Madrid 3–0 Valencia) and the first time post-2002 Madrid had a CL final to contend. Carlo Ancelotti won his third European Cup — 2003 and 2007 with AC Milan, 2014 with Real Madrid; he is the only manager (along with Bob Paisley) to have won it three times and the fifth manager to win it with two clubs (Ernst Happel, Ottmar Hitzfeld, José Mourinho, Jupp Heynckes, now Ancelotti). Atlético lost, in agony, the match they led for 92 minutes; the wait for a second European Cup final ended in the worst possible manner — a late header and three extra-time blows.
