## The short answer

The 2000 European Championship final was played at De Kuip in Rotterdam, the Netherlands, on 2 July 2000. World champions France beat Italy 2-1 via a golden goal in extra time. Referee: Anders Frisk (Sweden). Attendance: 48,100. Thierry Henry was named man of the match.

## The road to the final

France finished second in a group with hosts the Netherlands, Denmark and the Czech Republic, then beat Spain in the quarter-final and Portugal in the semi-final through a Zidane golden goal. Italy won Group B with maximum points, beat Romania in the quarter-final, and progressed past the Netherlands on penalties in the semi-final.

## How the match unfolded

Italy led through Marco Delvecchio's 56th-minute goal and looked set to win until France equalised in stoppage time through Sylvain Wiltord. In extra time, David Trezeguet half-volleyed into the top corner for a golden goal, sealing a 2-1 comeback and France's second European title.

## What the final meant

- France's second European Championship, and the first team to hold the World Cup and European title at the same time.
- A dramatic comeback in the dying seconds, one of the most remarkable finishes in tournament history.
- Trezeguet's golden goal became one of the most celebrated in the competition's history.

## Match details

The final was refereed by Anders Frisk of Sweden before 48,100 spectators at De Kuip in Rotterdam on 2 July 2000. France's semi-final against Portugal was decided by a golden goal from Zinedine Zidane, while Italy's semi-final against the Netherlands went to a penalty shoot-out. The late equaliser by Wiltord in the fourth minute of stoppage time forced extra time, where Trezeguet's half-volley ended the contest. France had won the World Cup two years earlier on home soil, and this victory made them the first reigning world champions to also lift the European Championship.
