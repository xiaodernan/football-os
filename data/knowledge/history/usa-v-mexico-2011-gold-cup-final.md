## The short answer

The 2011 CONCACAF Gold Cup final was played at the Rose Bowl in Pasadena, California, on 25 June 2011. Mexico beat the United States 4-2. Referee: Joel Aguilar (El Salvador). Attendance: 93,420.

## The road to the final

For a third consecutive final, the USA and Mexico contested the trophy, with Mexico the defending champions. It was Mexico's seventh Gold Cup final and the USA's eighth, and their fourth in succession.

## How the match unfolded

The United States led 2-0 through Michael Bradley and Landon Donovan, but Mexico scored four times to win 4-2. Pablo Barrera netted the first and third, Andrés Guardado the second and Giovani dos Santos the fourth, completing a remarkable comeback.

## What the final meant

- Mexico's sixth Gold Cup title and a second consecutive defence of the trophy.
- A comeback from two goals down in a final, echoing their 2009 rout of the USA.
- qualification for the 2013 FIFA Confederations Cup in Brazil.
- Only the second time in twelve years the USA lost to Mexico on home soil.

The final at the Rose Bowl in Pasadena on 25 June 2011 was refereed by El Salvador's Joel Aguilar before 93,420 spectators. The USA, in a fourth straight final, went 2-0 up through Bradley and Donovan and looked set to lift the trophy. Mexico, managed by José Manuel de la Torre, then produced a stunning comeback: Barrera scored the first and third, Guardado the second and dos Santos the fourth, for a 4-2 win.
