## The short answer

The 2004–05 UEFA Champions League final was played at Atatürk Olympic Stadium in Istanbul on 25 May 2005, the first European final ever held in Turkey — all-Spanish officiating led by Manuel Mejuto González, attendance 69,000. AC Milan led 3–0 at halftime, goals from Paolo Maldini (1', becoming the oldest scorer in European Cup final history) and Hernán Crespo (39' and 44'). Liverpool scored three times in six minutes after the break — Steven Gerrard (54', a lofted header from John Arne Riise's cross), Vladimír Šmicer (56', a low long-range shot), and Xabi Alonso (60', who had his penalty saved by Dida but scored the rebound) — to draw 3–3. Extra time produced no goals; Andriy Shevchenko twice found room in the 117th minute and was denied twice by Jerzy Dudek, who described the second save as "the save of my life". Liverpool won the penalty shootout 3–2: Serginho missed over the bar, Dietmar Hamann scored, Andrea Pirlo was saved by Dudek, Djibril Cissé and Jon Dahl Tomasson scored, Riise was saved by Dida, Kaká scored to level 2–2, Šmicer scored, and Ševchenko's decisive penalty was blocked by Dudek — shot straight down the middle, Dudek pushed it away with his left hand. Player of the match: Steven Gerrard.

## Route to the final

Liverpool entered through the third qualifying round as fourth-placed finishers in the 2003–04 Premier League, beating GAK 2–1 on aggregate (2–0 at Graz with two Gerrard goals, losing the second leg 0–1 at Anfield). In Group A they finished second to defending finalists Monaco, 10 points to Monaco's 12 — a 25-yard half-volley from Gerrard in the 86th minute against Olympiacos on the final group matchday sent them through. The knockout route was brutal in the right order: Bayer Leverkusen 6–2 on aggregate in the round of 16 (3–1 in each leg), Juventus 2–1 on aggregate in the quarter-finals (2–1 at home, 0–0 at Turin), Chelsea 1–0 on aggregate in the semi-final — Luis García's away goal at Stamford Bridge being the "ghost goal" José Mourinho never accepted. Milan, who had won the competition six times (1963, 1969, 1989, 1990, 1994, 2003) and were appearing in their tenth final — second in three years — won Group F with 13 points ahead of Barcelona (10), eliminated Manchester United 2–0 on aggregate in the round of 16 (1–0 in each leg), Inter Milan 5–0 on aggregate in the quarter-finals (2–0 at home, 3–0 in Milan), and survived PSV Eindhoven 3–3 on aggregate in the semi-final on away goals (2–0 at home, 1–3 at Eindhoven in the second leg). The final paired a domestic champion Milan against a Liverpool that had not won the league yet had won the continent before and had never missed again when the road turned — Liverpool had previously lifted the European Cup in 1977, 1978, 1981 and 1984; Heysel cost them six years outside Europe; this was their first final since 1985 and their sixth appearance.

## What the final meant

It was the second time in three years a Champions League final finished on penalties (Milan had beaten Juventus 3–2 in the 2003 final at Old Trafford), and extra time in a final for the 13th time in the competition's history. For Liverpool it was a fifth European Cup (1977, 1978, 1981, 1984, 2005), a template for how an English club could win Europe without winning the league first. For Gerrard it became the benchmark for captain-led comeback nights: first scorer in open play, catalyst of the second, Player of the match. For Milan and Ancelotti the Istanbul wound stayed with the club, though it was closed in 2007 with a 2–1 win in Athens, anchored by Filippo Inzaghi twice — a retribution the club deserved but on a night without the Istanbul drama. For Benítez, it was the ultimate proof that a fourth-placed Premier League side could cross the qualifying bridge and the biggest stage in May. And for the competition itself, the Atatürk night reset what a halftime deficit could mean inside football's biggest match — the result stopped being the scoreline at halftime and started being the scoreline at fulltime and the shootout afterwards.
