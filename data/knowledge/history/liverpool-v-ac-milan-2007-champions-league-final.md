## The short answer

The 2006–07 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Olympic Stadium, Athens, Greece, on 23 May 2007 at 21:45 — partly cloudy, 24 °C (75 °F), 46% humidity, before 63,000 witnesses. AC Milan beat Liverpool 2–1, winning their seventh Champions League (1963, 1969, 1989, 1990, 1994, 2003, 2007) in their eleventh final — the third final in five years. Goals: Filippo Inzaghi 45' (a Pirlo free-kick deflected off him into the net), Filippo Inzaghi 82', Dirk Kuyt 89'. Referee: Herbert Fandel (Germany), assisted by Carsten Kadach and Volker Wezel; Florian Meyer fourth official. UEFA Man of the Match: Filippo Inzaghi.

This was the rematch of the 2005 final at Istanbul, where Liverpool had trailed 0–3 at half-time, levelled at 3–3, and won the shoot-out. Here the outcome went the other way, and by full-time the streak was Milan's.

## What the route told us

Both teams entered in the third qualifying round, and both routes were tortuous.

Milan finished third in the 2005–06 Serie A but were docked 30 points for Calciopoli; the original punishment would have barred them entirely, and it was reduced on appeal to a third-place finish that still granted a Champions League qualifying slot. They beat Red Star Belgrade 1–0 (home) and 2–1 (away) in the third qualifying round. Group H: AEK Athens 3–0 home and 1–0 away loss; Anderlecht 1–0 away and 4–1 home; Lille 0–0 away and 2–0 home; Milan finished top on 10 points (Lille 9, AEK Athens 8, Anderlecht 4).
Knockouts: Celtic 1–0 on aggregate — 0–0 at Celtic Park, 1–0 at the San Siro after 90 scoreless minutes with Kaká scoring three minutes into extra time past Artur Boruc; Bayern Munich 4–2 on aggregate — 2–2 at the San Siro (Bayern equalised three minutes into stoppage time on the first leg), 2–0 at the Allianz Arena (two first-half goals); Manchester United 5–3 on aggregate — a first leg at Old Trafford where Kaká scored twice to put Milan 2–1 up after Ronaldo had scored early, before Wayne Rooney's second-half brace gave United 3–2, then a 3–0 second leg at the San Siro.

Liverpool finished third in the 2005–06 Premier League and entered in the third qualifying round as well, playing Maccabi Haifa at Anfield audiences of 2–1 (H) and 1–1 away in Kyiv (the tie was played in Kyiv because Israel was not deemed a safe venue that season; the game was played at Lobanovskyi Dynamo Stadium). They topped Group C with 13 points (PSV Eindhoven 10, Bordeaux 7, Galatasaray 4).
Knockouts: Barcelona 2–2 on aggregate, through to the next round on the away goals rule — Liverpool had won 2–1 away at the Camp Nou (Craig Bellamy and John Arne Riise scored the comeback after Deco had given Barcelona the lead), Barcelona won 1–0 at Anfield through a Lionel Messi goal; quarter-finals PSV Eindhoven 4–0 on aggregate (3–0 at the Philips Stadion, 1–0 at Anfield); semi-finals Chelsea 1–1 on aggregate, 4–1 on penalties — Chelsea won 1–0 at Stamford Bridge through a first-half Joe Cole goal, Liverpool won 1–0 at Anfield through a Daniel Agger goal, penalties went 4–1 to Liverpool.

## How the match decided itself

Milan won the toss; Liverpool kicked off. The 4–2–3–1 Milan shape, the oldest starting eleven ever in a Champions League final (average age 31 years, 34 days — the Maldini of the record books included) — Paolo Maldini at 38 years and 331 days became the oldest outfield player ever to play in a final in his eighth appearance in one. Milan wore their traditional all-white "lucky shirt"; Liverpool wore red.

The opening phase belonged to Liverpool in stretches — Pennant, Gerrard, Kuyt probing — but the late free-kick decided the first half. A defensive slip late in the first half gave Liverpool an attack on the edge, and a free-kick was taken by Pirlo with the ball drifting full and in — it deflected off Inzaghi's shoulder into the Liverpool net beyond Pepe Reina. The referee did not award a handball; replays showed the ball had hit Inzaghi's upper arm. Inzaghi later said the deflection was deliberate, the arm was not. Milan took 1–0 into the break.
Goal 2 came eight minutes from full-time: Kaká unlocked the Liverpool back line, Inzaghi collected and finished across Pepe Reina. Kuyt pulled one back in the 89th minute — a header from a Pennant corner flicked on by Agger — but full-time came before a second.

## What the final meant

It was Milan's seventh European Cup in eleven finals — the competition's oldest winning line. Ancelotti's job had been openly under threat during a poor December run; the Athens victory made him safe. Kaká finished the campaign as the competition's top scorer with ten goals. The win obliged Milan to play the 2007 UEFA Super Cup against Sevilla — they won 3–1, in a match overshadowed by the death of Sevilla's Antonio Puerta earlier in the summer, which raised the possibility that the Super Cup might not go ahead. It also obliged them to play the 2007 FIFA Club World Cup: they beat Urawa Red Diamonds 1–0 in the semi-final and Boca Juniors 4–2 in the final, Milan's first ever FIFA Club World Cup.

The post-match was shaped by a ticketing scandal: only 9,000 of 63,800 tickets for the Olympic Stadium were for general sale; 34,000 had gone to the clubs, 20,800 to UEFA and sponsors; thousands of fake tickets circulated and up to 5,000 fans holding genuine tickets were turned away after the stadium hit capacity; Greek riot police used tear gas and batons at the perimeter. UEFA initially branded Liverpool's supporters the "worst fans in Europe" through the Gaillard spokesman line — a claim Michel Platini later denied. UEFA were criticised for insufficient ticket-checking procedures.

## The wider meaning

- Ancelotti would later add Real Madrid's 2014 and 2022 victories to his ledger, becoming the first manager in history to win the competition four times (2003, 2007, 2014, 2022).
- It ended Liverpool's streak of European Cup finals within their home era. Their next appearance in a final would be against Real Madrid in Kyiv 2018 — a defeat.
- The trophy was presented to the winning captain Paolo Maldini by UEFA President Michel Platini in the Guest of Honour's box, returning the trophy to the captain in the stands — a reversion to pre-podium tradition. It was the first time UEFA's president had played against him in a competitive match in the Juventini tradition.
