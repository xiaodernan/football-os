## The short answer

The 1984–85 European Cup final was played at the Heysel Stadium, Brussels, on 29 May 1985 at 20:15 CEST — kicked off after a delay due to pre-match violence — before 58,000 witnesses. Liverpool 0–1 Juventus. Goal: Michel Platini 56' or 58' (the penalty was awarded in the 56th; the goal was scored then — Wikipedia's match summary places it in the 56th, the details table in the 58th's); Referee André Daina (Switzerland).

The match is inseparable from the Heysel disaster that occurred before it: 39 people died, 600 were injured. English clubs were banned from European competition for five years. UEFA extended the ban appropriately, with Liverpool carrying an extra three-year penalty upon the general resumption — which is why Liverpool did not return to the European Cup until 2005.

## What the route told us

Liverpool: First round: Lech Poznań 5–0 agg (1–0 A, 4–0 H, hat-trick from John Wark); Second round: Benfica 3–2 agg (3–1 H, 0–1 A); Quarter-finals: Austria Wien 5–2 agg (1–1 A, 4–1 H); Semi-finals: Panathinaikos 5–0 agg (4–0 H, 1–0 A).

Juventus: First round: Ilves 6–1 agg (4–0 A, 2–1 H — Paolo Rossi hat-trick, Platini scoring); Second round: Grasshopper 6–2 agg (2–0 H, 4–2 A); Quarter-finals: Sparta Prague 3–1 agg (3–0 H, 0–1 A); Semi-finals: Bordeaux 3–2 agg (3–0 H, 0–2 A).

## How the match decided itself

The first half was 0–0. In the 56th minute a penalty was awarded against Liverpool, after Boniek broke through and Gillespie was adjudged to have brought him down — reportedly well outside the area — Platini kept his nerve and the final outcome was not in question after that moment.

Juventus 1–0. Liverpool's last European final until 2005.

## What the final meant

Juventus completed their treble of the European Cup, UEFA Cup and Cup Winners' Cup — the first club to do so. For Liverpool, it marked the end of a golden generation; Joe Fagan retired; the tragic day at Heysel was something the sport could never un-live.
