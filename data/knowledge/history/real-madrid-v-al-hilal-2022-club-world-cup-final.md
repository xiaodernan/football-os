## The short answer

The 2022 FIFA Club World Cup final was played at Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium in Rabat, Morocco, on 11 February 2023. Real Madrid beat Al-Hilal 5-3. Referee: Anthony Taylor (England). Attendance: 44,439. Vinícius Júnior was named man of the match.

## The road to the final

This was the 19th FIFA Club World Cup final, contested by the champions of the six confederations and the host nation's league champions. Real Madrid entered as UEFA Champions League winners; Al-Hilal represented the AFC as Asian champions. Carlo Ancelotti's side had won the 2022 Champions League final, while Ramón Díaz's team arrived from the Saudi Pro League.

## How the match unfolded

Vinícius Júnior opened the scoring in the 13th minute and Federico Valverde added a second in the 18th. Moussa Marega pulled one back in the 26th. Karim Benzema restored the two-goal cushion in the 54th, but Luciano Vietto scored twice in the 63rd and 79th minutes to set up a tense finish. Valverde struck again in the 58th and Vinícius completed his brace in the 69th, sealing a 5-3 win.

## What the final meant

- Real Madrid's fifth FIFA Club World Cup title and eighth recognised club world championship.
- Eight goals made it the highest-scoring Club World Cup final in the competition's history.
- A fifth consecutive Club World Cup crown for UEFA representatives, extending Europe's run in the tournament.
- A landmark for Asian club football: Al-Hilal became the first AFC club to score three goals in a Club World Cup final.

## Match details

The final at Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium in Rabat on 11 February 2023 was refereed by England's Anthony Taylor before 44,439 spectators, with China's Ma Ning serving as fourth official. Real Madrid took a 2-0 lead through Vinícius Júnior and Valverde inside eighteen minutes, only for Marega to halve it before half-time. Benzema made it 3-1 in the 54th minute, Valverde restored the cushion four minutes later, and Vietto's 63rd-minute goal kept Al-Hilal alive. Vinícius struck again in the 69th before Vietto's second in the 79th completed the scoring at 5-3. Vinícius, whose pace and finishing had decided the match, was named man of the match. Madrid collected a fifth Club World Cup trophy; Al-Hilal left with the distinction of having scored more goals than any losing finalist in the tournament's history.
