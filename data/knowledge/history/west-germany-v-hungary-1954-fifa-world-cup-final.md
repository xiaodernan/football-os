## The short answer

The 1954 FIFA World Cup final was played at the Wankdorf Stadium in Bern, Switzerland, on 4 July 1954, in heavy rain. West Germany beat the heavily favoured Hungary 3-2 - a result known in German football lore as the "Miracle of Bern". Goals: Max Morlock 10', Helmut Rahn 18' and 84' (West Germany); Ferenc Puskas 6', Zoltan Czibor 8' (Hungary). Referee: William Ling (England). Attendance: 62,500.

## The road to the final

The two sides met in the group stage, where Hungary thrashed West Germany 8-3. Hungary, unbeaten for 31 matches and the Olympic champions, beat Brazil 4-2 in the quarter-final and defending champions Uruguay 4-2 after extra time in the semi-final. West Germany, who had rested key players in that 8-3 defeat, beat Yugoslavia 2-0 and Austria 6-1 in the knockout rounds.

## How the match unfolded

Hungary raced into a 2-0 lead - Puskas scoring after six minutes and Czibor after eight. West Germany pulled one back through Morlock on 10 minutes and levelled through Rahn on 18. Hungary controlled long spells and created many chances, but West Germany held firm. With six minutes left, Rahn drove low past the goalkeeper for 3-2. A late Puskas effort was ruled offside and the whistle confirmed the upset.

## What the final meant

- West Germany's first World Cup title, the first of four.
- The end of Hungary's Golden Team unbeaten run and their best-ever World Cup finish.
- A result that became a powerful symbol of post-war German recovery.
- Played in "Fritz Walter weather" - the rain in which the German captain thrived.
