## The short answer

The 1962–63 European Cup final was played at Wembley Stadium, London, on 22 May 1963 at 15:00 BST — before 45,715 witnesses. SL Benfica 1–2 AC Milan. Goals: Eusébio 19' for Benfica; José Altafini 58', 66' for Milan. Referee Arthur Holland (England).

It was the first European Cup final without a Spanish club (Real Madrid reached six of the previous seven finals, Barcelona the 1961 one against Benfica). Milan lifted their first European Cup — their redemption after the 3–2 loss to Real Madrid in the 1958 final. Nereo Rocco became the first Italian coach to win the European Cup.

Milan: Serie A champions (1961–62). Preliminary round: Union Luxembourg 14–0 agg (8–0 H, 6–0 A) — one of the most lopsided two-legged ties in the history of the competition; First round: Ipswich Town 4–2 agg (3–0 H, 1–2 A); Quarter-finals: Galatasaray 8–1 agg (3–1 A, 5–0 H); Semi-finals: Dundee 5–2 agg (5–1 H, 0–1 A).

## How the match decided itself

Eusébio gave Benfica the lead in the 19th minute. Milan equalized through Altafini on 58 minutes; eight minutes later, Altafini added his second, and Milan led 2–1. Benfica chased for the remaining minutes, but Milan held on for their first European Cup.

## What the final meant

This was the first of Milan's seven European Cups. For Benfica, it was the first of their three final defeats in the 1960s (1963, 1965) and the beginning of a long gap before 1988 and 1990. Nereo Rocco's win opened the Italian European decade — Inter won the next year (1964) and Milan would again win it in 1969.
