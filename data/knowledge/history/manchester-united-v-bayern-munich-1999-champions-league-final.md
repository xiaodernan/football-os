## The short answer

The 1998–99 UEFA Champions League final was played at Camp Nou in Barcelona on 26 May 1999 at 20:45 CEST — before 90,245 witnesses. Manchester United 2–1 Bayern Munich. Goals: Mario Basler 6' (Bayern Munich), Teddy Sheringham 90+1', Ole Gunnar Solskjær 90+3' (Manchester United). Referee Pierluigi Collina (Italy). Assistant referees Gennaro Mazzei and Claudio Puglisi; fourth official Fiorenzo Treossi.

It was Manchester United's second European Cup/Champions League title (their first since 1968) and completed a historic treble with the Premier League and FA Cup won earlier that season.

## How the match decided itself

Basler gave Bayern Munich the lead in the 6th minute with a low free kick. Bayern held that advantage deep into the match, with Manchester United struggling to find a clear route back despite long spells of pressure. In stoppage time, substitutes changed the ending: Sheringham turned in from close range in the 90+1st minute to level, and Solskjær diverted in the winner in the 90+3rd minute from a corner. In a few minutes, a final Bayern seemed to control became Manchester United's.

## What the final meant

- **Manchester United's treble** — Champions League, Premier League and FA Cup in the same 1998–99 season.
- **A second European crown** — United's first European Cup since 1968.
- **Stoppage-time reversal** — one of the most dramatic finishes in a European Cup/Champions League final.
- **Bayern's near miss** — Bayern had led from the 6th minute and came within moments of the title.
