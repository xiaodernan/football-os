## The short answer

The 1961–62 European Cup final was played at the Olympisch Stadion, Amsterdam, Netherlands, on 2 May 1962 at 19:30 CEST — before 61,257 witnesses. SL Benfica 5–3 Real Madrid. Goals: Benfica — José Águas 25', Domiciano Cavém 33', Mário Coluna 50', Eusébio 64' (pen.), 67'; Real Madrid — Ferenc Puskás 18', 23', 39'. Referee Leo Horn (Netherlands).

It was the second-highest-scoring European Cup final (eight goals), behind only the 1960 Hampden classic (ten). Puskás' treble was the first hat-trick scored in a European Cup final for the losing side. Eusébio's brace (one a penalty) announced him to Europe.

## What the route told us

Benfica: defending champions, entered with a bye. First round: Austria Wien 6–2 agg (1–1 A, 5–1 H); Quarter-finals: 1. FC Nürnberg 7–3 agg (1–3 A, 6–0 H); Semi-finals: Tottenham Hotspur 4–3 agg (3–1 H, 1–2 A). Spain kept the home leg — the away-goals rule did not yet exist, but Benfica won the tie on aggregate anyway.

Real Madrid: Preliminary round: Vasas of Hungary 5–1 agg (2–0 A, 3–1 H); First round: Boldklubben 1913 of Denmark 12–0 agg (3–0 A, 9–0 H); Quarter-finals: Juventus 1–1 across two legs (1–0 H, 0–1 A) — replay at a neutral venue (Paris), 3–1; Semi-finals: Standard Liège 6–0 agg (4–0 H, 2–0 A).

## How the match decided itself

Puskás gave Real Madrid the lead 18 minutes in — and his second came 23 minutes in. Águas pulled one back in the 25th. Cavém equalised 33 minutes in. Puskás completed his hat-trick with his third in the 39th minute for Madrid, outscoring what they had. The half ended with Madrid defending the 3–2 score.

In the second half, Coluna levelled the match on 50 minutes; ten minutes later Eusébio converted from the penalty spot (64') to put Benfica 4–3 ahead; and three minutes after that, Eusébio added the fifth. Benfica ran out 5–3 winners and retained the trophy; Real Madrid's European decade (1956–1960, perennial finalists and winners) was formally over.

## What the final meant

This was Benfica's second consecutive European Cup — the second club (after Real Madrid) to defend the trophy, and Portugal's only back-to-back European Cup. Eusébio — then 20 years old — was the European Footballer of the Year three seasons later (1965), the first African-born winner. Benfica returned to finals in 1963, 1965, 1968, 1988 and 1990, but never lifted the trophy again.
