## The short answer

The 2005–06 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Stade de France, Saint-Denis (Paris), France, on 17 May 2006 at 20:45 — partly cloudy, 15 °C, 33% humidity, before 79,610 witnesses. Barcelona beat Arsenal 2–1. Goals: Sol Campbell 37' (headed home from a Thierry Henry free-kick after Carles Puyol fouled Emmanuel Eboué), Samuel Eto'o 76' (from Henrik Larsson's one-touch lay-off), Juliano Belletti 80' (Larsson one-two, near-post). Referee was Terje Hauge (Norway). UEFA Man of the Match was Samuel Eto'o.

Jens Lehmann was sent off in the 18th minute — the first man sent off in a European Cup final — and Arsenal held a one-goal lead for almost the whole match with ten men. Two late goals became four unforgettable minutes. Barcelona lifted the trophy a fortnight after winning La Liga; their second European Cup after 1992.

## What the route told us

Arsenal entered as Group B winners on 16 points (Ajax 11, Thun 4, Sparta Prague 2), unbeaten and conceding no goals in their ten previous Champions League games (919 minutes since they had last conceded in the group stage; two goals shipped across the whole campaign before the final). Knockouts: Real Madrid 1–0 (1–0 away at the Bernabéu, 0–0 at Highbury); Juventus 2–0 (both goals at Highbury); Villarreal 1–0 (1–0 home, 0–0 away) to reach their first ever final and London's first.

Barcelona entered as Group C winners on 16 points (Werder Bremen 7, Udinese 7, Panathinaikos 4) and unbeaten in the tournament, with 114 goals in all competitions that season. Knockouts: Chelsea 3–2 on aggregate (a 2–1 first-leg away win at Stamford Bridge; 1–1 at the Camp Nou in the return leg); Benfica 2–0 on aggregate; Milan 1–0 on aggregate (1–0 at the San Siro, 0–0 at the Camp Nou).

## How the match decided itself

Arsenal won the coin toss; Barcelona kicked off. Their red home kit clashed with Arsenal's, so Arsenal wore yellow and Barcelona the traditional blue and claret. Jens Lehmann was sent off after 18 minutes for bringing down Eto'o outside the box — the first dismissal in European Cup final history. Manuel Almunia was brought on in goal. Arsenal hung on to half-time with ten men; Barcelona's best first-half chance was an Eto'o shot that struck the post via Almunia's save.

Arsenal nonetheless scored first in the 37th minute: the free-kick was won by Emmanuel Eboué, Henry delivered it, and Campbell headed it at the near post. Almunia then saved from Iniesta early in the second half; everything shifted in the space of four minutes after Henrik Larsson's arrival in the 61st minute. For the equaliser, Larsson peeled off the inside-left channel, met Iniesta's pass and slipped a one-touch ball into Eto'o; for the winner, he played a one-two with Belletti down the inside-right channel and Belletti slid it low past Almunia through his legs at the near post.

## What the final meant

Barcelona won their second European Cup after 1992, and their fifth final appearance; the defeat for Arsenal ended a ten-match shutout run that had been the competition's most impressive defensive season. Barcelona had the ratified numbers — a fortnight earlier they had retained the La Liga title as champions. It was also the first final played with the new trophy, since Liverpool kept the 2005 one.

Arsenal: first European Cup final in the club's history and London's first; their ten-match shutout run ended at the worst moment. Lehmann's red was the single biggest talking point of that final — reviewed in every pundit panel since, never overturned. Barcelona's 2006 squad was the direct ancestor of the 2009 treble winners: Puyol captained, Márquez, van Bronckhorst, Valdés, Iniesta, Xavi — Ronaldinho's last European final as a starter.

The final's broader aftermath: Barcelona lost the Super Cup to Sevilla 3–0 in August 2006 and the FIFA Club World Cup final 0–1 to Internacional (Brazil) in December.
