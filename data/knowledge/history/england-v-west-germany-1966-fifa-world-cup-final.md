## The short answer

The 1966 FIFA World Cup final was played at Wembley Stadium in London on 30 July 1966. England beat West Germany 4-2 after extra time to win the Jules Rimet Trophy. Referee: Gottfried Dienst (Switzerland). Attendance: 96,924. Geoff Hurst scored a hat-trick — the first ever in a World Cup final.

## The road to the final

England, managed by Alf Ramsey and captained by Bobby Moore, won Group 1 without conceding a goal: 0-0 with Uruguay, 2-0 with Mexico, 2-0 with France. They then beat Argentina 1-0 in the quarter-final and Portugal 2-1 in the semi-final. West Germany, under Helmut Schön and captained by Uwe Seeler, won Group 2 and knocked out Uruguay 4-0 and the Soviet Union 2-1. England won the toss and chose to kick off.

## How the match unfolded

Helmut Haller put West Germany ahead in the 12th minute after Ray Wilson's misdirected clearance. Geoff Hurst headed England level in the 18th minute from a Bobby Moore free kick. Martin Peters made it 2-1 in the 78th minute; Wolfgang Weber equalised in the 89th to force extra time. In the 101st minute Hurst swivelled and shot against the underside of the crossbar; the ball bounced down and referee Dienst, after consulting linesman Tofiq Bahramov, awarded the goal. Hurst completed his hat-trick in the 120th minute, with spectators already coming onto the pitch.

## What the final meant

- England's first and, to this day, only World Cup title, and the country's first major international trophy.
- Hurst's hat-trick stood alone in World Cup finals until Kylian Mbappé scored three in the 2022 final.
- The disputed third goal entered the language: in Germany a goal that bounces off the crossbar is still called a *Wembley-Tor*.
- The British television audience peaked at 32.3 million, then the most-watched event in UK television history.

## Match details

Wembley Stadium, London, 30 July 1966, 15:00 BST. Attendance 96,924. Referee Gottfried Dienst (Switzerland); linesmen Tofiq Bahramov (Soviet Union) and Karol Galba (Czechoslovakia). England: Gordon Banks; George Cohen, Jack Charlton, Bobby Moore (captain), Ray Wilson; Nobby Stiles, Alan Ball, Bobby Charlton, Martin Peters; Roger Hunt, Geoff Hurst. West Germany: Hans Tilkowski; Willi Schulz, Horst-Dieter Höttges, Wolfgang Weber, Karl-Heinz Schnellinger; Helmut Haller, Franz Beckenbauer, Wolfgang Overath; Lothar Emmerich, Uwe Seeler (captain), Sigfried Held. Ninety minutes, thirty of extra time, no substitutions permitted. Peters was the only player booked.
