## The short answer

The UEFA Euro 2020 final was played at Wembley Stadium in London on 11 July 2021. Italy drew 1-1 with England after extra time and won 3-2 on penalties. Referee: Björn Kuipers (Netherlands). Attendance: 67,173. Leonardo Bonucci was named man of the match.

## The road to the final

Italy reached their fourth European Championship final, having topped Group A and beaten Austria, Belgium, Spain and England's conquerors on the way. England were playing in their first Euro final and only their second major tournament final after the 1966 World Cup. Roberto Mancini's side had not lost a match in the tournament; Gareth Southgate's team had not conceded a goal until the final.

## How the match unfolded

Luke Shaw scored after just 119 seconds, the fastest goal ever in a European Championship final, giving England the lead. Leonardo Bonucci equalised in the 67th minute, and the match stayed 1-1 through extra time. In the shoot-out, England led 2-1 after two kicks each, but Marcus Rashford, Jadon Sancho and Bukayo Saka all missed, while Italy converted three of five to win 3-2.

## What the final meant

- Italy's first major title since the 2006 World Cup and their first European Championship since 1968.
- Italy drew level with France on two European Championship titles, one behind Spain and Germany.
- England became the third nation this century to lose a European Championship final on home soil, after Portugal in 2004 and France in 2016.
- An estimated global audience of 328 million watched, despite Covid-reduced attendance.

## Match details

The final at Wembley on 11 July 2021 was refereed by the Netherlands' Björn Kuipers before a Covid-limited crowd of 67,173. England struck almost immediately: Shaw's low finish after 119 seconds remains the fastest goal in the tournament's final. Italy pressed for the rest of the match and Bonucci's header from a corner in the 67th minute levelled the score. Extra time produced no further goal. In the shoot-out, Kane and Maguire scored for England, while Berardi, Bonucci and Bernardeschi scored for Italy; Belotti and Jorginho missed for Italy, but Rashford, Sancho and Saka all missed for England, handing Italy the trophy. Bonucci, whose goal had kept Italy in the match, was named man of the match.
