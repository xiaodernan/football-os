## The short answer

The 2020–21 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Estádio do Dragão, Porto, Portugal, on 29 May 2021 — clear night, 19 °C, 72% humidity, before 14,110 witnesses. Chelsea beat Manchester City 1–0 in the third all-English final of the competition's history. Goal: Kai Havertz 1–0, late in the first half. Referee was Antonio Mateu Lahoz (Spain). UEFA Man of the Match was N'Golo Kanté. It was Chelsea's second Champions League title (after 2012's against Bayern at Munich), and Thomas Tuchel had become the first manager to reach the final in consecutive seasons with different clubs (losing the 2020 final with Paris Saint-Germain). Manchester City were playing in their first European Cup final; they were the ninth distinct English side to reach this stage.

The final was played at the Dragão because UEFA had originally selected Saint Petersburg, then moved it to Istanbul in June 2020 — after the 2020 final had already been moved to Lisbon because of the COVID-19 pandemic. Two weeks before kick-off, UEFA relocated it again — to Porto, a "green list" country for England-based fans — to allow a limited number of supporters to attend, permitting 14,110 through the gates.

## What the route told us

Chelsea came through Group E as winners with 14 points; knockout: Atlético Madrid 3–0 agg (1–0 in Madrid, 2–0 at Stamford Bridge); Porto 2–1 agg (2–0 in Porto, 0–1 at Stamford Bridge after extra-time goals); Real Madrid 3–1 agg (1–1 at the Bernabéu, 2–0 at Stamford Bridge). Manchester City came through Group C as winners with 16 points; knockout: Borussia Mönchengladbach 4–0 agg (two legs at the Puskás Aréna, Budapest); Borussia Dortmund 4–2 agg; Paris Saint-Germain 4–1 agg (2–1 in Paris, 2–0 at City).

Earlier in the season both clubs had been within reach of each other constantly: City won 3–1 at Stamford Bridge in the league; Chelsea came back to win 2–1 at the Etihad; Chelsea won 1–0 in the FA Cup semi-final in April 2021, denying City the chance of a quadruple. The two clubs had also met twice in the FA Community Shield finals of 2012 and 2018 (City winning both) and in the 2019 EFL Cup final (City winning on penalties after a 0–0 draw at Wembley).

## How the match decided itself

The match kicked-off shortly after 8pm in front of 14,110. Havertz opened the scoring late in the first half. The second half saw City press in vain, booked through Rüdiger at the hour mark, denied by late blocks and clearances; both captains substituted late; Mahrez almost drew City level late in stoppage time. The fourth official put up a minimum of seven minutes of added time; City's best chance in the closing moments deflected behind from a Mahrez effort. Chelsea closed it down and held the gate.

## What the final meant

It was Chelsea's second Champions League win and their first since 2012. Thomas Tuchel became the first manager to reach the final in consecutive seasons with different clubs — losing the 2020 final to Bayern Munich with Paris Saint-Germain, winning with Chelsea. It was the third all-English final in the history of the competition, after the 2008 Moscow final and the 2019 Madrid final, and remains — as of the published record — the most recent final between teams from the same association. Chelsea went on to win the UEFA Super Cup and the FIFA Club World Cup.

This was the fourth European Cup/Champions League final played in Portugal (after 1967's Estádio Nacional, and 2014 and 2020 at the Estádio da Luz), and it was the first to be staged outside Lisbon — with 14,110 supporters permitted into the 50,000-seat Dragão at 33% capacity. Chelsea went on to win the 2021 UEFA Super Cup (against Villarreal) and the 2021 FIFA Club World Cup (against Palmeiras). City's quadruple chase ended in the FA Cup semi-final against Chelsea itself; City went on without them to the 2023 UEFA Champions League final, which they won against Inter Milan in Istanbul.
