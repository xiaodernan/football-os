## The short answer

The 2000–01 UEFA Champions League final was played at San Siro, Milan, on 23 May 2001 at 20:45 — mostly cloudy, 22 °C, 64% humidity, before 79,000 witnesses. Bayern Munich 1–1 Valencia after 120 minutes; Bayern won the penalty shoot-out 5–4. Both goals of the match were penalties: Gaizka Mendieta 3' (Valencia), Stefan Effenberg 50' (Bayern). Referee Dick Jol (Netherlands); assistants Jaap Pool and Jan-Willem van Veluwen; fourth official Jan Wegereef. UEFA Man of the Match: Oliver Kahn.

It was the second European Cup final to be decided on penalties — and the second for a team Garcia-led by Héctor Cúper, who had already lost the 2000 final with Valencia and would lose twice in a row. Bayern lifted their fourth European Cup; it was their first in 25 years.

## What the route told us

Bayern: Group F winners on 11 points (Paris Saint-Germain 10, Rosenborg 7, Helsingborgs 5) — then Group C winners on 13 points (Arsenal 8, Lyon 8, Spartak Moscow 4). Knockouts: Manchester United 3–1 on aggregate (1–0 away at Old Trafford, 2–1 at home); semi-finals Real Madrid 3–1 on aggregate (1–0 away at the Bernabéu, 2–1 at home).

Valencia: Group C winners on 12 points (Lyon 9, Olympiacos 9, Heerenveen 4) — then Group A winners on 12 points (Manchester United 12, Sturm Graz 6, Panathinaikos 2). Knockouts: quarter-finals Arsenal 2–2 on aggregate (away 1–2, home 1–0 — advanced on the away-goals rule); semi-finals Leeds United 3–0 on aggregate (0–0 away at Elland Road, 3–0 home at the Mestalla).

## How the match decided itself

Mendieta opened the scoring with a penalty in the third minute — awarded after Patrik Andersson was deemed to have handled the ball in the penalty area. Minutes later Bayern were awarded a penalty — Jocelyn Angloma had fouled Stefan Effenberg in the penalty box — but Santiago Cañizares saved Mehmet Scholl's kick with his legs. In the 50th minute, Amedeo Carboni was deemed to have handled the ball in the penalty box contesting a header with Carsten Jancker. Effenberg took the kick and scored, sending Cañizares the wrong way.

The scores remained level through the rest of regular time and remained so through extra time. The shoot-out went the full distance. Bayern won 5–4, and Oliver Kahn lifted the trophy with a right-hand save to deny Mauricio Pellegrino's final kick. Kahn also received the UEFA Fair Play Award for consoling the defeated Cañizares afterwards.

The shoot-out went to sudden death and concluded with Kahn making the final save to deny Mauricio Pellegrino — Bayern 5–4.

## What the final meant

It was Bayern's fourth European Cup, their first since 1976 and their first in 25 years. Their manager Ottmar Hitzfeld joined Ernst Happel as the only two managers to win the European Cup with two clubs — after Borussia Dortmund in 1997. Valencia lost their second final in two years, their only finals in the competition.
