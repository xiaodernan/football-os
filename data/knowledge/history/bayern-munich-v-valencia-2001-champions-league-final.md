## The short answer

The 2000–01 UEFA Champions League final was played at the San Siro in Milan on 23 May 2001 at 20:45 CEST — before 79,000 witnesses. Bayern Munich 1–1 Valencia after extra time; Bayern Munich won 5–4 on penalties. Goals: Gaizka Mendieta 3' pen (Valencia), Stefan Effenberg 50' pen (Bayern Munich). Referee Dick Jol (Netherlands). Assistant referees Jaap Pool and Jan-Willem van Veluwen; fourth official Jan Wegereef. Oliver Kahn was named man of the match.

It was Bayern Munich's fourth European Cup/Champions League title and their first since 1976. Valencia lost a second consecutive Champions League final.

## How the match decided itself

Mendieta converted a penalty in the 3rd minute to give Valencia the lead. Bayern were awarded a penalty soon after, but Santiago Cañizares saved Mehmet Scholl's kick. Early in the second half Bayern won another penalty, and Effenberg scored to make it 1–1. The score stayed level through extra time.

In the shoot-out, Bayern won 5–4. Paulo Sérgio missed first for Bayern while Mendieta scored; Salihamidžić and Carew converted; Zickler scored and Zahovič was saved; Andersson was saved and Carboni's effort struck the bar; Effenberg and Baraja scored; Lizarazu and Kily González scored; Linke converted and Kahn saved Pellegrino's decisive kick.

## What the final meant

- **Bayern's fourth European title** — their first European Cup/Champions League crown since 1976.
- **Valencia's second straight final defeat** — after losing in 2000, Valencia fell again in 2001.
- **Hitzfeld's second club, second title** — Ottmar Hitzfeld won the competition with Dortmund in 1997 and Bayern in 2001.
- **Kahn's night** — decisive saves in the shoot-out defined the final and earned him man of the match.
