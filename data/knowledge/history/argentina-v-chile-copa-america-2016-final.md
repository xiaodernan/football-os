## The short answer

The Copa América Centenario final was played at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey, on 26 June 2016. Argentina drew 0-0 with Chile after extra time and Chile won 4-2 on penalties. Referee: Héber Lopes (Brazil). Attendance: 82,026. Claudio Bravo was named man of the match.

## The road to the final

This was the 45th Copa América final and a rematch of the 2015 final. Argentina and Chile had already met in Group D, where Argentina won 2-1. Argentina beat Venezuela and the United States in the knockout rounds; Chile beat Mexico and Colombia, the latter after a long weather delay in Chicago.

## How the match unfolded

Neither side could break the deadlock through ninety minutes or extra time. Chile took the first penalty and converted; Argentina's Lionel Messi missed the first kick for his side. Argentina missed two spot-kicks in total while Chile converted four, winning the shoot-out 4-2 and retaining the Copa América.

## What the final meant

- Chile's second consecutive Copa América title, won on penalties for the second year running.
- A third consecutive major final defeat for Argentina and Lionel Messi.
- After the match, Messi announced his retirement from international football; he later reversed the decision.
- Claudio Bravo, whose saves and composure steadied Chile, was named man of the match.

## Match details

The final at MetLife Stadium on 26 June 2016 was refereed by Brazil's Héber Lopes before 82,026 spectators. The match was a tense, goalless contest: ninety minutes and extra time produced no goal. In the shoot-out Chile went first and converted, then watched Messi miss Argentina's opening kick. Argentina missed two of their penalties; Chile converted four to win 4-2. Bravo, the Chilean goalkeeper, was named man of the match. It was Chile's second straight Copa América crown, both secured via penalty shoot-outs against the same opponent. For Messi, it was a third consecutive final defeat with Argentina; in the immediate aftermath he announced his international retirement, a decision he subsequently reversed.
