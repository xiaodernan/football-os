## The short answer

The 2014 FIFA World Cup final was played at the Maracanã Stadium in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, on 13 July 2014 — before 74,738 witnesses. Germany 1–0 Argentina after extra time. Goal: Mario Götze 113' (Germany). Referee Nicola Rizzoli (Italy). Mario Götze was named man of the match.

It was Germany's fourth FIFA World Cup title and their first since 1990 (and first as reunified Germany). Argentina were runners-up for the third time in their World Cup history.

Germany (Group G winners): beat Portugal 4–0, drew 2–2 with Ghana, and beat the United States 1–0. Knockout stage: Algeria 2–1 after extra time (round of 16), France 1–0 (quarter-final), Brazil 7–1 (semi-final).

Argentina (Group F winners): beat Bosnia and Herzegovina 2–1, Iran 1–0, and Nigeria 3–2. Knockout stage: Switzerland 1–0 after extra time (round of 16), Belgium 1–0 (quarter-final), Netherlands 0–0 after extra time, 4–2 on penalties (semi-final).

## How the match decided itself

The final was tense and finely balanced through normal time. Gonzalo Higuaín missed a clear first-half chance for Argentina, and Benedikt Höwedes struck the post for Germany shortly before the interval. Lionel Messi also had an opportunity early in the second half but could not convert. With no breakthrough after 90 minutes, the match moved to extra time.

In the 113th minute, André Schürrle drove down the left and delivered a cross into the area. Mario Götze controlled on his chest and finished with his left foot to give Germany the decisive 1–0 lead, which they held to the end.
