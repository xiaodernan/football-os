## The short answer

The 2001–02 UEFA Champions League final was played at Hampden Park, Glasgow, Scotland, on 15 May 2002 at 19:45 — mostly cloudy with rain showers, 15 °C, before 50,499 witnesses. Bayer Leverkusen v Real Madrid finished 1–2. Goals: Raúl 8', Lúcio 14', Zinedine Zidane 45'. Referee: Urs Meier (Switzerland). UEFA Man of the Match: Zinedine Zidane. A minute of silence was held for Valeriy Lobanovskyi, the Dynamo Kyiv coach who had died two days earlier.

Zidane's 45th-minute left-footed volley from Roberto Carlos's cross into the top corner is regarded as one of the greatest goals in Champions League history. It settled a final that had been even for its first 45 minutes and was never even after.

## What the route told us

Real Madrid qualified as Group A winners (13 points — Roma 9, Lokomotiv 7, Anderlecht 3), then Group C winners (16 points — Panathinaikos 8, Sparta Prague 6, Porto 4) and knocked out the defending champions Bayern Munich 3–2 on aggregate (1–2 away, 2–0 home) and then Barcelona 3–1 on aggregate in the semi-finals (2–0 at the Camp Nou in the first leg, 1–1 at the Bernabéu in the second).

Bayer Leverkusen qualified as Group F runners-up behind Barcelona (Barcelona 15, Leverkusen 12) after a 3–0 aggregate win over Red Star Belgrade in the qualifying stage, then Group D winners (10 points — Deportivo La Coruña 10, Arsenal 7, Juventus 7). Knockouts: Liverpool 4–3 on aggregate (0–1 away at Anfield, 4–2 home — one of the most astonishing quarter-finals ever), and Manchester United 3–3 on aggregate with away goals in the semi-final (2–2 away first leg at Old Trafford, 1–1 home).

## How the match decided itself

Real Madrid took the lead in the 8th minute through Raúl. Leverkusen equalised in the 14th minute through Lúcio. The match remained open until Zidane — who had carried Leverkusen's defenders for the half — collected a Roberto Carlos cross and volleyed left-footed into the top corner on the 45th minute. It is the defining image of the Zidane–Madrid era.

Half-time: 1–2 Madrid. Leverkusen pressed; Madrid absorbed and countered. César was replaced by Iker Casillas in goal at 68' (César had picked up a knock mid-match). Leverkusen's near-misses were not enough; 90 minutes: 1–2. Real Madrid's ninth European Cup.

## What the final meant

It was Zidane's only European Cup win — and Real Madrid's ninth, in their 12th final (1956, 1957, 1958, 1959, 1960, 1962, 1964, 1966, 1981, 1998, 2000). Leverkusen completed a run of three finals lost in the same season: Bundesliga (Bayern Munich finished 8 points clear), DFB-Pokal (to Schalke) and the Champions League (top by weeks before the final, the title had slipped at the run-in).

Five Leverkusen starters from the final added silver medals at the 2002 World Cup (Ballack, Butt, Neuville, Ramelow, Schneider); Lúcio lifted the World Cup as the only Leverkusen player in Brazil's squad.

## The wider meaning

- It was Real Madrid's ninth European Cup — their first under the galáctico era, and the first of three Zidane-adjacent Madrid European Cups that began with his summer of 2001 (they had also just won the World Championship of Clubs late 2002).
- It confirmed Leverkusen's anti-miracle era: a team that lost three finals in three weeks.
- Roberto Carlos's cross — delivered from deep on the left — is the template for the modern dead-cross winger.
