## The short answer

The 1986 FIFA World Cup final was played at the Estadio Azteca in Mexico City on 29 June 1986. Argentina beat West Germany 3-2. Goals: Jose Luis Brown 23', Jorge Valdano 55', Jorge Burruchaga 84' (Argentina); Karl-Heinz Rummenigge 74', Rudi Voller 81' (West Germany). Referee: Romualdo Arppi Filho (Brazil). Attendance: 114,600. The Azteca thus became the first stadium to host two World Cup finals (1970 and 1986).

## The road to the final

Argentina topped Group A (two wins, one draw) and beat Uruguay in the round of 16, England in the quarter-final and Belgium in the semi-final. West Germany finished second in Group E and beat Morocco, hosts Mexico on penalties, and France in the semi-final.

## How the match unfolded

Jose Luis Brown put Argentina ahead with a header in the first half. Jorge Valdano doubled the lead after the interval with a low drive. West Germany fought back - Rummenigge pulled one back in the 74th minute and Voller equalised with a header in the 81st, setting up a tense finale. Three minutes later Jorge Burruchaga broke into the box and fired into the corner to seal a 3-2 win.

## What the final meant

- Argentina's second World Cup title, sixteen years after their first in 1978.
- Afitting climax to Diego Maradona's tournament, widely regarded as one of the greatest individual performances in World Cup history.
- The Estadio Azteca became the first ground to stage two World Cup finals.
- West Germany's second consecutive final defeat, after losing in 1982.
