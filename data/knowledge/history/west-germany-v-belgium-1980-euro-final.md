## The short answer

The UEFA Euro 1980 final was played at the Stadio Olimpico in Rome on 22 June 1980. West Germany beat Belgium 2-1. Referee: Nicolae Rainea (Romania). Attendance: 47,860. Horst Hrubesch scored both German goals; René Vandereycken converted a Belgian penalty.

## The road to the final

Belgium, managed by Guy Thys and captained by Julien Cools, topped their qualifying group and won Group 2 at the finals, which contained hosts Italy, England and Spain: 1-1, 2-1 and 0-0. West Germany, under Jupp Derwall and captained by Bernard Dietz, won qualifying Group 7 and topped finals Group 1 with two wins and a draw: 1-0 against Czechoslovakia, 3-2 against the Netherlands — Klaus Allofs scoring all three — and 0-0 with Greece. It was their third consecutive European final.

## How the match unfolded

In the 10th minute Bernd Schuster chipped over Eric Gerets to Hrubesch, who controlled the ball on his chest and half-volleyed it into the far corner. Belgium almost levelled immediately; François Van der Elst shot over. Schuster and Allofs drew saves from Jean-Marie Pfaff. In the 75th minute Stielike fouled Van der Elst, and Vandereycken scored the penalty. With ninety seconds left, Rummenigge's corner found Hrubesch making a late run; he headed in from close range to settle it.

## What the final meant

- West Germany's second European Championship in three attempts, twelve years after their 1972 win.
- Hrubesch scored both goals in the final and later recalled the heat: lifting the trophy was difficult because he was so tired.
- Rummenigge, who assisted the first goal with a corner and took the second, won the 1980 Ballon d'Or.
- Six West Germany players and one Belgian made UEFA's team of the tournament.

## Match details

Stadio Olimpico, Rome, 22 June 1980, 20:30 CEST. Attendance 47,860. Referee Nicolae Rainea (Romania). Belgium: Jean-Marie Pfaff; Eric Gerets, Walter Meeuws, Luc Millecamps, Michel Renquin; Julien Cools (captain), Wilfried Van Moer, René Vandereycken; Raymond Mommens; François Van der Elst, Jan Ceulemans. West Germany: Toni Schumacher; Uli Stielike, Bernard Dietz (captain), Karlheinz Förster; Manfred Kaltz, Hans-Peter Briegel; Bernd Schuster, Hansi Müller; Karl-Heinz Rummenigge; Horst Hrubesch, Klaus Allofs. Bernhard Cullmann replaced Briegel after an ankle injury.
