## The short answer

The 2011–12 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Allianz Arena (“Fußball Arena München” in official materials that night, since UEFA does not recognise a sponsor on the arena name) in Munich, Germany, on 19 May 2012 at 20:45 local time under partly cloudy 20 °C weather in front of 62,500 spectators. Bayern Munich and Chelsea drew 1–1 after extra time; Chelsea won the penalty shootout 4–3 — their first European Cup title, the first for a London club, the fifth for an English club, and the 22nd in competition history. Scorers: Bayern's Thomas Müller 83' (Toni Kroos cross; his header bounced off the turf and over Čech's crossbar), Chelsea's Didier Drogba 88' (Juan Mata's corner, near-post header past Manuel Neuer). In extra time Bayern were awarded a penalty for Drogba's foul on Franck Ribéry; Arjen Robben took it and Petr Čech saved the low drive. Referee Pedro Proença (Portugal); Player of the match: Didier Drogba; Fans' player of the match: Petr Čech.

## How the match was played

Bayern dominated possession and the chances: Robben sliced into the box twice; Gómez fired over the bar; Müller's late header put Bayern ahead in the 83rd minute — Kroos's swinging cross met Müller's stooping header, the ball bounced off the turf and in over Čech's reach. With Bayern ahead and protecting the trophy they had missed two years earlier, the evening looked decided. But Chelsea earned their first corner of the night in the 88th minute, Mata swung it in, and Drogba met the ball at the near post to beat Neuer — 1–1. Chelsea then had a free kick outside the area in the 93rd minute, but Drogba smashed his effort over the bar. In extra time no goals separated the teams: Robben missed Bayern's penalty; Olić attempted to set up Van Buyten instead of taking a shot and the ball rolled wide of Čech's far post; Bayern never scored again, and the decisive hour fell to the penalty shootout as described already above.

The shootout: Lahm opened for Bayern and struck low to Čech's left; Mata replied for Chelsea but his soft effort was saved by Neuer; Gómez drilled into the bottom-right corner (Bayern 1–1 Chelsea); David Luiz punished Bayern with a run-up power shot into the top corner (Chelsea 1–2); Neuer (Bayern) beat Čech with a low drive into the bottom-left corner (2–2); Lampard levelled high and central (3–2); Olić took his final competitive kick for Bayern but Čech saved to his left (3–3); Cole levelled at 3–3 with a shot low and right; Schweinsteiger was Bayern's fifth taker, the same player who had converted the clinching penalty against Real Madrid in the semi-finals; Čech smiled one way and tipped the ball onto the post; Drogba sent Neuer the wrong way to win it — Chelsea 4–3.

## What the route told us

Bayern came through Group A as winners — 13 points; they opened the tournament by beating Zürich 3–0 on aggregate in the play-off round (2–0 at home, 1–0 away), then topped their group and eliminated Basel 7–1 on aggregate in the round of 16 (after losing 1–0 at St. Jakob-Park), Marseille 4–0 in the quarter-finals (2–0 away, 2–0 home), and Real Madrid 3–3 on aggregate in the semi-finals (2–1 at home, defeat 1–2 away in extra time; Bayern won the shoot-out 3–1). Chelsea, by contrast, needed chaotic comebacks at every elimination stage: after topping Group E on 11 points with an away draw at Valencia and an away loss at Leverkusen, they scraped through Napoli 5–4 on aggregate (1–3 away; 4–1 at Stamford Bridge in extra time), Benfica 3–1 (1–0 away; 2–1 home), and Barcelona 3–2 (1–0 at home; 2–2 in Barcelona).

This was Bayern's ninth European Cup final. Chelsea's second (first time was 2008 in Moscow in an all-English affair) — and their first title. Bayern had won four previous trophies (1974, 1975, 1976, 2001) and lost four (1982, 1987, 1999, 2010). Several records were set: this was the fourth final played at a finalist's own home ground; the fourth in competition history played by two teams English versus German; the last final of any European competition not to feature a domestic champion since 2007; and Bayern's first CL final in Germany since 1997 (when Munich had hosted the 2–1 defeat to Borussia Dortmund at the Olympiastadion).

## What the final meant

For Chelsea this was the founding club's first European Cup — the first London club to win the trophy, ending twenty years of Champions League target-hunting. For Bayern this loss at home was a record ninth final of losses: four trophies won, four lost; the 1999 comeback agony and this one carry the same hurt for Bayern's folklore. For the competition it was the last final in which none of the participating clubs was then domestic champion, the fourth final on home soil in history and the first under the Champions League name, and a reminder that the methodical battering of Bayern by a side that reached the final without involving themselves much in the search for balance could hold a desperately lean evening together.
