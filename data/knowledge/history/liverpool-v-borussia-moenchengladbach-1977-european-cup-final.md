## The short answer

The 1976–77 European Cup final was played at the Stadio Olimpico, Rome, on 25 May 1977 at 20:15 CEST — before 52,078 witnesses. Borussia Mönchengladbach 1–3 Liverpool. Goals: Allan Simonsen 52' for Gladbach; Terry McDermott 28', Tommy Smith 65', Phil Neal 83' (pen.) for Liverpool. Referee Robert Wurtz (France).

It was Liverpool's first European Cup. Bob Paisley — who had inherited Liverpool from Bill Shankly — became the first manager to win the UEFA Cup (1976) and then the European Cup (1977) in successive seasons. Mönchengladbach reached a first European Cup final, and would remain a great German side throughout the decade.

## How the match decided itself

The match began with Gladbach creating the first chance — Rainer Bonhof struck the woodwork early on. Liverpool settled quickly: in the 28th minute, Callaghan fed Heighway down the right; Heighway cut inside and passed into space for McDermott, whose finish opened the scoring for the Reds.

Seven minutes after the restart, Allan Simonsen ran onto Jimmy Case's stray pass and equalised at 1–1 (52').

In the ten minutes after Simonsen's equaliser, Vogts seemed to trip Keegan in the penalty area — the referee waved play on.

The decisive passage came in the 65th minute: Heighway took a corner from the left; Tommy Smith met it at the near post and headed it into the far corner — his 600th appearance for Liverpool. Eighteen minutes later, Vogts brought Keegan down again in the penalty area — this time the referee ordered a penalty, and Phil Neal converted. Liverpool's European Cup, at last, in Rome.

## What the final meant

This was an era-defining moment for the club: Liverpool brought home their first European Cup — after the 1973 UEFA Cup (defeating Mönchengladbach on aggregate), the 1976 UEFA Cup (defeating Brugge). Bob Paisley inherited the side from Bill Shankly, organised it further, and produced European glory in his first season as manager. Emlyn Hughes captained; Keegan, Heighway, Callaghan, McDermott, Smith formed an era band.
