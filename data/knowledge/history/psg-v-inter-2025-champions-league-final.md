## The short answer

The 2024–25 UEFA Champions League final, played on 31 May 2025 at the Allianz Arena in Munich, was won by Paris Saint-Germain 5–0 against Inter Milan. It was PSG's first European Cup title; it was also the second French club's, after Marseille in 1993, and the largest winning margin in any of the main European men's club competitions final. PSG had finished fifteenth in the league phase — the deepest route any European Cup winner has taken through the new format — yet won every knockout tie along the way.

## How the match was played

Vitinha threaded a pass to Désiré Doué early; Doué squared it to Achraf Hakimi at the back post, and the defender swept in first to make it 1–0 in the 12th minute. Eight minutes later, Ousmane Dembélé picked out Doué on the left; the teenager's volley took a decisive touch off Federico Dimarco, crossing the line in the 20th minute to complete a ruthless first half. Inter managed their first shot on target by the 76th minute. The second half was a demonstration: in the 63rd minute, Dembélé played in Vitinha, whose return found Doué once more for the third; in the 73rd, Dembélé released Kvaratskhelia to break the line and score one-on-one for four; in the 86th, Senny Mayulu — three minutes after coming on — scored for five.

## What the route told us

PSG finished 15th in the league phase — thirteen points from eight matches — and needed the play-off to reach the last 16, then turned Liverpool around at Anfield (1–1 on aggregate, won 4–1 on penalties), Aston Villa over two legs (5–4 on aggregate) and Arsenal in the semi-finals (3–1 on aggregate). Inter had finished fourth with nineteen points, then beat Feyenoord 4–1, Bayern Munich 4–3 and Barcelona 7–6 on aggregate after extra time. The two roads taught the same lesson the final taught: the long route can be the winning route, provided the team never concedes.

## What the final meant

PSG completed the continental treble — Ligue 1, Coupe de France, Champions League — the first French club to do it, and the first since Marseille in 1993 to lift Europe's highest prize. Luis Enrique became the second manager, after Pep Guardiola, to win the continental treble twice (having previously completed it with Barcelona in 2014–15). Désiré Doué was named man of the match for his brace, Senny Mayulu the second-youngest scorer in a Champions League final, and Simone Inzaghi left Inter by mutual consent three days after the final. A year later, in the 2026 final at the Puskás Aréna in Budapest, PSG defended the crown: they drew 1–1 with Arsenal after extra time and won 4–3 on penalties.

## What the final taught the reader

The 2025 final was not a result; it was a demonstration. The team that scores early stays high and punishes an opponent's build-up block for a full ninety minutes; the team that scores five in a final does so because each of its positions — keeper, back line, midfield, front line — did its job in the same half. The record is there for any analyst willing to read it, not just to watch it.
