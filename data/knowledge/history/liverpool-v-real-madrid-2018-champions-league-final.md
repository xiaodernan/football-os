## The short answer

The 2017–18 UEFA Champions League final was played at the NSC Olimpiyskiy Stadium, Kyiv, Ukraine, on 26 May 2018 at 21:45 — sunny, 20 °C, 37% humidity, before 61,561 witnesses. Real Madrid beat Liverpool 3–1 in their third consecutive Champions League final, and the fourth of five seasons. Goals: Karim Benzema 41' (Carvajal cross), Sadio Mané 51' (equaliser), Gareth Bale 61' (an overhead kick from a Marcelo cross) and 80' (long range). Referee was Milorad Mažić (Serbia). UEFA Man of the Match was Gareth Bale.

Real Madrid were the first team in the Champions League era to win three consecutive finals. Bale's overhead kick is regarded as one of the greatest goals in a final — Gareth Bale, who was on for only the build-up from the bench, came on around the hour mark and produced two goals within a short window, both his first in the competition that season.

## What the route told us

Real Madrid qualified via Group H runners-up (13 points) before three back-to-back knockout ties: Paris Saint-Germain 5–2 aggregate; Juventus 4–3 aggregate; Bayern 4–3 aggregate — compounding three 90-minute wins at home after road trouble everywhere. Liverpool qualified through the play-offs after Manchester City's league route — they came through Hoffenheim, all of Group E, Porto, Manchester City 5–1 agg and Roma 7–6 agg.

## What the final meant

Real Madrid won their 13th European Cup overall — and the third Champions League title in three years (2016, 2017, 2018). Zinedine Zidane became the first manager in the Champions League era to win three titles from three finals, and the only player-manager combination to manage it in the CL era: Zidane won 2002 as a player, and then 2016, 2017, 2018 as head coach. Gareth Bale scored his only two Champions League goals this season against Liverpool, both in this final; he was a substitute off the bench in the 60th minute for Isco.

Liverpool's first European Cup final since losing to AC Milan in 2007 — their eighth European Cup final, first in the Champions League era since 2007, first not at Wembley in twenty years. Liverpool were the first team since Bayern Munich in 2012 to reach this final after entering the qualifying playoffs. Zinedine Zidane, who won 2002 as a player, had now won three consecutive titles as manager.
