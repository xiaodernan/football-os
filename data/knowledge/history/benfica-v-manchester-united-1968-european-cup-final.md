## The short answer

The 1967–68 European Cup final was played at Wembley Stadium, London, on 29 May 1968 at 19:45 BST — before 92,225 witnesses. SL Benfica 1–4 Manchester United after extra time. Goals: Jaime Graça 79' for Benfica; Bobby Charlton 53', 99' in the extra period; George Best 92' and Brian Kidd 94' on his 19th birthday for Manchester United. Referee Concetto Lo Bello (Italy).

This was Manchester United's first European Cup final — ten years after the Munich air disaster claimed eight of their players (Charlton and Foulkes were survivors). It was the first European Cup won by an English club. Brian Kidd scored on his 19th birthday in the final itself; Man of the Match was John Aston.

## What the route told us

Manchester United (English champions): First round: Hibernians 4–0 agg (4–0 H; another 0–0 away at the Hibernian Stadium, Edinburgh) — David Sadler and Denis Law scored two each; Second round: Sarajevo 2–1 agg; Quarter-finals: Górnik Zabrze 2–1 agg (2–0 H; 0–1 away in the snow at the Arena Zabrze); Semi-finals: Real Madrid 4–3 agg (1–0 H, 3–3 A at the Bernabéu) — a passage to the final that made the dream concrete.

Benfica (Portuguese champions): First round: Glentoran of Northern Ireland 1–1 (away goals) — Eusébio's 86' goal saved Benfica at Belfast; Second round: Saint-Étienne 2–1 agg (2–0 H, 0–1 A); Quarter-finals: Vasas 3–0 agg; Semi-finals: Juventus 3–0 agg (2–0 H, 1–0 A).

## How the match decided itself

United went close first — Best was felled six times, twice by Humberto, who was booked on 20 minutes. Aston had two shots saved by Henrique in the second half; Charlton in the 53rd minute met Dunne's cross at the far post with a header to give United the lead. Graça equalised on 79 minutes after Augusto and Torres built it. Extra time: Best collected the long goal-kick from Stepney, dribbled past the defence and past the goalkeeper to roll into an empty net (92'); Sadler's effort was saved by Henrique but the rebound fell to Kidd, who headed it in (94'); Charlton completed the scoring in the 99th, finishing Kidd's pass with his second of the night.

## What the final meant

Two seasons ran in parallel: Celtic had lifted the Cup in Lisbon a year earlier; United became the first English club to win it. Benfica reached their fifth final and lost again — the third defeat of the decade (1963, 1965, 1968). Ten years after Munich, the two surviving United players — Bobby Charlton and Bill Foulkes — lifted the trophy; Charlton was the talisman; George Best scored one of the three goals in the extra period; Brian Kidd scored on his 19th birthday, the same day the trophy was handed over.
