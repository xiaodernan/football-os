## The short answer

The 1994 FIFA World Cup final was played at the Rose Bowl in Pasadena, California, on 17 July 1994. Brazil beat Italy 3-2 on penalties after a 0-0 draw through extra time - the first World Cup final decided by a shoot-out, and the only one goalless after 120 minutes. Referee: Sandor Puhl (Hungary). Attendance: 94,194.

## The road to the final

Brazil topped Group B and beat Russia in the round of 16, the Netherlands in the quarter-final and Sweden in the semi-final. Italy advanced from a group of death and beat Nigeria, Spain and Bulgaria in the knockout rounds.

## How the match unfolded

The final was an attritional, goalless contest in the heat. Both sides had chances - Italy's Roberto Baggio and Brazil's Romario came close - but neither scored in 120 minutes. In the shoot-out, Italy's Franco Baresi and Baggio both missed (Baggio sending his effort over the bar), while Brazil converted three of five. Brazil's win was dedicated to Formula One champion Ayrton Senna, who had died two and a half months earlier.

## What the final meant

- Brazil's fourth World Cup title, their first since 1970, and the first team to lift the new FIFA World Cup Trophy.
- The first final decided on penalties, a format that would become familiar in later tournaments.
- A memorial to Ayrton Senna, whose death shadowed the Brazilian squad throughout the tournament.
- The last World Cup final played in broad daylight until 2026.
