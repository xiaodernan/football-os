## The short answer

The 2003–04 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Arena AufSchalke, Gelsenkirchen, Germany, on 26 May 2004 at 20:45 — before 53,053 witnesses. AS Monaco 0–3 FC Porto. Goals: Carlos Alberto 39', Deco 71', Dmitri Alenichev 75'. Referee Kim Milton Nielsen (Denmark); assistants Jens Larsen and Jørgen Jepsen; fourth official Knud Erik Fisker. UEFA Man of the Match: Deco.

It was F.C. Porto's second European Cup (its first since 1987), and José Mourinho's first European Cup as a manager — a week later he had left for Chelsea.

## What the route told us

Monaco: Group C winners on 11 points (Deportivo La Coruña 10 — Monaco won 8–3 at home on Matchday 4, the highest-scoring CL game at the time; AEK Athens 2, PSV Eindhoven 10). Knockouts: Lokomotiv Moscow 2–2 on aggregate (1–2 away first leg, 1–0 home — advanced on away goals); quarter-finals Real Madrid 5–3 on aggregate (2–4 away at the Bernabéu, 3–1 home); semi-finals Chelsea 5–3 on aggregate (3–1 home, 2–2 away at Stamford Bridge).

Porto: Group F runners-up behind Real Madrid (Porto 11, Real Madrid 14, Marseille 4, Partizan 3). Knockouts: Manchester United 3–2 on aggregate (2–1 home, 1–1 away at Old Trafford — the last-minute Costinha equaliser that sent Mourinho into his finest hour yet); Lyon 4–2 on aggregate (2–0 home, 2–2 away); Deportivo La Coruña 1–0 on aggregate (0–0 home, 1–0 away).

## How the match decided itself

Carlos Alberto opened the scoring in the 39th minute after a turnover in midfield, driving into the area and finishing low. Monaco's attack — Pršo, Giuly, Rothen — could not break through; Porto's defence (Jorge Costa, Ricardo Carvalho) absorbed what pressure arrived. Deco doubled the lead in the 71st minute with a shot from outside the area; Alenichev added a third in the 75th minute.

Monaco had zero shots on target. That was the final's summary in one line — Porto's perfect organisation — Jorge Costa, Ricardo Carvalho at the back, Costinha in front of the defence, and Deco offering the breaks on the counter through Maniche, Nuno Valente and Paulo Ferreira on the flanks. This was the last Champions League final won by a club from outside the big five leagues (England, Spain, Italy, Germany, France).

## What the final meant

José Mourinho's first European Cup as a manager — a week later, he was being introduced at Chelsea. Deco had been the catalyst on the night. FC Porto's second European Cup; the Estádio do Dragão had only been open two years.

Porto's second European Cup launched another era for the club. Monaco were the fourth French club to reach a final (Reims 1956 and 1959, Saint-Étienne 1976, Marseille 1991 and 1993). Their 8–3 win over Deportivo La Coruña on matchday four was the highest-scoring Champions League match at the time — Pršo scored four, Giuly two, Rothen, Plašil and Cissé one each. The record stood until 2016-11-22, when Borussia Dortmund beat Legia Warsaw 8–4 in the group stage.

The 8–3 win over Deportivo had been Monaco's statement of attacking belief. This final was Porto's — ninety minutes in which Monaco produced zero shots on target. Porto's whole architecture was built around defence first and the line-splitting passes of Deco.
