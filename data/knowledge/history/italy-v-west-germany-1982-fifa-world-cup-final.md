## The short answer

The 1982 FIFA World Cup final was played at the Santiago Bernabeu in Madrid, Spain, on 11 July 1982. Italy beat West Germany 3-1. Goals: Paolo Rossi 57', Marco Tardelli 69', Alessandro Altobelli 81' (Italy); Paul Breitner 83' (West Germany). Referee: Arnaldo Cezar Coelho (Brazil). Attendance: 90,089.

## The road to the final

Italy survived a group of death and beat Argentina and Brazil in the second group stage, then Poland in the semi-final. West Germany topped their opening group and beat England, Spain and France (on penalties) before the final.

## How the match unfolded

A goalless first half gave way to an Italian rout. Paolo Rossi opened the scoring in the 57th minute from a Gentile cross. Marco Tardelli made it 2-0 in the 69th with a famous celebration, and Alessandro Altobelli added a third. Paul Breitner pulled one back late, but Italy's 3-1 win was never in doubt.

## What the final meant

- Italy's third World Cup title (1934, 1938, 1982), forty years after their last.
- Paolo Rossi won both the Golden Boot and Golden Ball, having scored six goals in the knockout stages.
- A definitive end to West Germany's golden generation's hopes.
