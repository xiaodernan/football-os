## The short answer

The 2004–05 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Atatürk Olympic Stadium, Istanbul, Turkey, on 25 May 2005 at 21:45 — clear night, 18 °C, 78% humidity, before 69,000 witnesses. It was the first European Cup final held in Turkey. AC Milan led 3–0 at half-time (Maldini 1', Crespo 39', Crespo 44'); Liverpool scored three times in the twelfth half between the 54th and 60th minutes (Gerrard 54', Šmicer 56', Alonso 60') to level at 3–3; the match went to extra time and a penalty shoot-out, and Jerzy Dudek saved Andriy Shevchenko's decisive penalty to give Liverpool a 3–2 shoot-out win — and their fifth European Cup.

Referee was Manuel Mejuto González (Spain). UEFA Man of the Match was Steven Gerrard. Attendance 69,000. The final is universally known as the Miracle of Istanbul.

## What the route told us

Liverpool entered in the third qualifying round after finishing fourth in the 2003–04 Premier League. They beat GAK Graz 2–1 on aggregate (2–0 away at Graz with two Gerrard goals, 0–1 home). Group A: runners-up behind 2004 finalists Monaco (Monaco 12, Liverpool 10, Olympiacos 10) — the defining moment of that group being Gerrard's 25-yard half-volley against Olympiacos at Anfield on 8 December 2004, 86th minute, "Oh, you beauty! What a hit son!" from co-commentator Andy Gray. Knockouts: Bayer Leverkusen 6–2 on aggregate (3–1 home, 3–1 away); Juventus 2–1 on aggregate (2–1 home, 0–0 away); Chelsea in the semi-final — 0–0 away at Stamford Bridge, then 1–0 at Anfield through García's 111th-minute goal, a shot no camera conclusively showed crossing the line — José Mourinho dubbed it the "ghost goal", and admitted afterwards that he felt the power of Anfield.

Milan topped Group F with 13 points (Barcelona 10, Shakhtar 6, Celtic 5) and beat Manchester United 2–0 on aggregate (1–0 at Old Trafford, 1–0 at the San Siro); Inter Milan 5–0 on aggregate (2–0, 3–0); PSV Eindhoven 3–3 on aggregate (2–0 home, 1–3 away) and advanced on the away-goals rule.

## How the match decided itself

Milan won the coin toss. Maldini volleyed in after one minute — then the oldest scorer in European Cup history — from a Pirlo free-kick, conceded by Djimi Traoré. Crespo doubled it in the 39th minute (Kaká to the far post for the pass) and made it 3–0 in the 44th (another Kaká assist — chip over Dudek).

Benítez changed formation at half-time: Hamann came on for Finnan, switching to a 3–4–2–1. The response was historic: Gerrard connected with Riise's cross and lofted a header over Dida in the 54th minute; Šmicer beat Dida with a long-range shot in the 56th; a minute later Gerrard won a penalty — Gattuso brought him down after Baros' lay-off, Alonso's penalty was saved by Dida, but Alonso scored the rebound for 3–3 on the hour.

Extra time produced no goals. The defining night belonged to Dudek: in the 117th minute he saved Shevchenko point-blank, then saved again from inside 3 yards — he called it "the save of my life". Penalties: Serginho over, Hamann scored (broken toe) for Liverpool, Pirlo saved by Dudek, Cissé scored, Tomasson scored, Riise saved by Dida, Kaká scored, Šmicer scored — and Shevchenko, who had beaten Buffon in the 2003 shoot-out, hit his penalty straight down the middle; Dudek blocked it with his left hand. Liverpool won 3–2.

## What the final meant

Liverpool won their fifth European Cup and, by the rules then in force, kept the trophy permanently as fifth-time winners — the fifth and final club to receive that honour (after Real Madrid, Ajax, Bayern and Milan). They earned the multiple-winner badge as well.

It was Liverpool's first European Cup since 1984. It was Milan's tenth final, their second in three years (2003). The teams met again in the 2007 final in Athens (Milan won 2–1) — both chapters are now in this basket.

Rafael Benítez admitted stuningly he had no words. The trophy tour through Liverpool drew around one million on the streets. The asterisk: Liverpool had finished a place outside the Champions League places in the Premier League (fifth). UEFA initially said four places per country; Everton were in then too; UEFA on 10 June 2005 confirmed both clubs would enter, Liverpool in the first qualifying round, and amended the rules so holders would defend their title automatically in future editions.

Benítez eventually stayed at Liverpool for seven years. Gerrard stayed — the Chelsea move was closed in July 2005 with a four-year renewal. Šmicer entered the final knowing it would be his last game, and scored.

Dudek's double save from Shevchenko topped a 2011 UEFA.com fan poll asking for the greatest Champions League moment of all time, ahead of Zidane's volley in the 2002 final and Solskjær's injury-time goal in the 1999 final.

## The wider meaning

- The first European final staged in Turkey; the Atatürk Olympic Stadium also hosted the 2023 Champions League final (Manchester City 1–0 Inter).
- The defining six minutes: 54', 56', 60'.
