## The short answer

The 2007–08 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Luzhniki Stadium, Moscow, Russia, on 21 May 2008 — cloudy, 14 °C, 96% humidity. It kicked off at 22:45 Moscow Time, the first European Cup final to start on one day and finish in the next. Manchester United beat Chelsea 6–5 on penalties after a 1–1 draw following extra time. Goals: Cristiano Ronaldo 26' (Wes Brown's cross from a throw-in, headed past Petr Čech — the first time United had scored in the first half of a European final), Frank Lampard 45' (a Michael Essien long-range shot deflected off Nemanja Vidić, then off Rio Ferdinand; Edwin van der Sar lost his footing and Lampard, running from deep, finished simply — he pointed to the sky twice, in memory of his mother Pat, who had died a month earlier). Referee was Ľuboš Micheľ (Slovakia). UEFA Man of the Match was Edwin van der Sar; Fans' Man of the Match was Cristiano Ronaldo. Attendance: 67,310 at the Luzhniki.

## How the match decided itself

Cristiano Ronaldo opened the scoring in the 26th minute from Wes Brown's right-flank cross — an interchange after a throw-in left Brown time to set up the headed finish. Chelsea nearly equalised in the 33rd when Lampard's cross was flicked back by Drogba and Ferdinand, under pressure from Michael Ballack, seemed forced into his own goal — van der Sar saved one-handed to keep the German at bay. Čech made a double save before half-time — Tevez's header from Ronaldo's cross, then Michael Carrick's loose-ball follow-up. Chelsea survived themselves before the final chance of the first half drifted their way.

Frank Lampard equalised in the final minute of the half. The sequence began with a long-range Essien strike, deflected first off Nemanja Vidić and then off Rio Ferdinand; van der Sar lost his footing as the ball changed direction, and Lampard, running from deep, finished simply. He pointed to the sky.

Chelsea transformed after the equaliser. Essien broke free in the 54th minute but curled his left-footed shot well over. Drogba struck the post on 77 minutes from 25 yards. Drogba came close to turning Joe Cole's low cross home four minutes from time. Sir Alex Ferguson introduced Ryan Giggs in place of Paul Scholes with seven minutes to play — Giggs's record 759th appearance. In extra time, Lampard struck the underside of the bar. Giggs stabbed his left-footed shot wide of a chance to sweep it with his right and had the follow-up headed away from the line by John Terry.

Late in the extra-time melee, the ball was put out of play for cramp treatment. Tevez put out the restart for another throw-in and summoned his teammates to press; Terry and Ballack reacted furiously; the melée drew assistant referee Martin Balko's attention. Balko saw Didier Drogba slap Nemanja Vidić, and Micheľ showed Drogba red for violent conduct — only the second red card in a European Cup final in history (Jens Lehmann, 2006, Arsenal). For the shoot-out, United brought on Anderson for Wes Brown; Chelsea replaced Makélélé with Juliano Belletti.

## The shoot-out, kick by kick

Manchester United won the coin toss and elected to shoot first, from the end housing their fans. Carlos Tevez opened the scoring, Čech going the wrong way. Michael Ballack replied for Chelsea. Michael Carrick converted. Juliano Belletti — on in the final minutes of extra time — scored with his first touch. Cristiano Ronaldo then missed United's third kick — stuttering through his run-up to throw Čech, only to see the keeper dive to his right. Frank Lampard converted Chelsea's third, putting them 3–2 ahead.

Owen Hargreaves rebalanced for United: 3–3. Ashley Cole converted for Chelsea — Van der Sar got a hand to the ball but could not keep it out — 4–3. Nani scored United's fourth to level the shoot-out: 4–4. John Terry stepped up for the fifth kick; he lost his footing as he planted his standing foot by the ball — even though van der Sar guessed the wrong way — his mis-hit effort struck the outside of the right post and went wide: 4–4.

Sudden death followed. Anderson — on the pitch for United since the 120th minute — scored with his first touch, not unlike Belletti. Salomon Kalou replied for Chelsea: 5–5. Ryan Giggs finished — 6–5. Nicolas Anelka stepped up for Chelsea needing to level. Van der Sar — who had pointed to his left before every penalty kick before him — dived to his right, and saved. The third European Cup in Manchester United's history: 1968, 1999, 2008.

## What the route told us

Manchester United came through Group F as winners with 16 points — the most of any group winner — ahead of Roma (11), Sporting CP (7) and Dynamo Kyiv (0). They reached the final unbeaten: nine wins and three draws, 19 goals scored, Cristiano Ronaldo seven. In the knockouts: Lyon 2–1 on aggregate (1–1 at the Gerland, 1–0 at Old Trafford); Roma 3–0 (2–0 in Rome — Ronaldo's first-half header, Rooney's second-half tap-in; 1–0 in Manchester); Barcelona 1–0 in the semi-finals (0–0 at the Camp Nou — Ronaldo's first-minute penalty struck the bar; 1–0 at Old Trafford, Paul Scholes on the 14th minute).

Chelsea won Group B with 12 points, ahead of Schalke 04 (8), Rosenborg (7) and Valencia (5). In the knockouts: Olympiacos 3–0 (0–0 in Athens; 3–0 at Stamford Bridge — Ballack, Lampard, Kalou); Fenerbahçe 3–2 on aggregate (1–2 at the Şükrü Saracoğlu in Istanbul — Deivid's nine-minutes-from-time winner; 2–0 at Stamford Bridge — Ballack, Lampard); Liverpool 4–3 after extra time in the semi-final (1–1 at Anfield — Kuyt before the break, Riise's own goal in the 95th minute; 3–2 at Stamford Bridge — two from Drogba, one from Lampard).

## What the final meant

The 2008 final was the first all-English final in the history of the competition — only the third final contested by clubs from the same country, after Real Madrid v Valencia 2000 (3–0 at the Stade de France) and Milan v Juventus 2003 (0–0 at Old Trafford, 3–2 on penalties). It was the first European Cup final played in Russia, the easternmost final in the competition's history, and the stage was the Luzhniki — opened in 1956 and the principal venue of the 1980 Summer Olympics.

For Manchester United, the third lift (1968, 1999, 2008) arrived in the centenary year of their first league triumph (1908), the fiftieth anniversary of the Munich air disaster (1958), and the fortieth of the 1968 European Cup. For Chelsea it was the first European Cup final in the club's history, ten days after they had lost the league title to United by two points; on 23 September 2007 they had lost to United 0–2 in Avram Grant's debut; on 26 April 2008 they had won 2–1 at Stamford Bridge. The 2007 FA Community Shield had already gone to United on penalties after a 1–1 draw.

The final also set up the two arcs of English club football that followed: Chelsea's late conversion to the European Cup in Munich, in 2012, under Roberto Di Matteo; and United's unfinished business a year later in Rome, losing 5–3 on penalties in the 2009 final. United went on to lose 2–1 to Zenit Saint Petersburg in the 2008 UEFA Super Cup, and then won the 2008 FIFA Club World Cup in Japan, beating LDU Quito 1–0.
