## The short answer

The 1964–65 European Cup final was played at the San Siro, Milan, Italy, on 27 May 1965 at 21:30 CET — before 89,000 witnesses. Inter Milan 1–0 Benfica. Goal: Jair 43'. Referee Gottfried Dienst (Switzerland).

This was Inter's second consecutive European Cup — the title Benfica had hoped to take back, having last seen in 1962.

This is the second and — as of 2026 — the last time a club won the European Cup in its own home stadium. Herrera's Inter completed the back-to-back at 21:30, and Benfica lost their third European Cup final in three years (1963, 1965) and the fifth in five (1961, 1962 wins).

Benfica: 1963–64 Primeira Divisão champions. Preliminary round: Aris Bonnevoie of Luxembourg 10–2 agg (5–1 A, 5–1 H); First round: La Chaux-de-Fonds of Switzerland 6–1 agg (1–1 A, 5–0 H); Quarter-finals: Real Madrid 6–3 agg (5–1 H, 1–2 A); Semi-finals: Vasas ETO Győr 5–0 agg (1–0 A, 4–0 H).

## How the match decided itself

Jair put Inter ahead on 43 minutes — a first-half strike after the Great Inter worked the Left flank through Facchetti. Benfica chased, centring on Eusébio, but Inter's catenaccio held firm. Inter went on to win 1–0 and retain the European Cup.

## What the final meant

This was Inter's second European Cup — they remain the only Italian club to defend the trophy (1964, 1965). Benfica, who had reached three finals in four years (1961 W, 1962 W, 1963 L), lost again; their golden age would pause until 1988.
