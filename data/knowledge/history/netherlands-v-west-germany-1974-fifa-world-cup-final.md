## The short answer

The 1974 FIFA World Cup final was played at the Olympiastadion in Munich, West Germany, on 7 July 1974. Hosts West Germany beat the Netherlands 2-1. Goals: Johan Neeskens 2' (penalty, Netherlands), Paul Breitner 25' (penalty), Gerd Muller 43' (West Germany). Referee: Jack Taylor (England). Attendance: 78,200.

## The road to the final

The Netherlands topped Group 3 and swept the second group stage, beating Argentina, East Germany and Brazil. West Germany finished second in Group 1, then beat Yugoslavia, Sweden and Poland in the second group stage.

## How the match unfolded

The Netherlands struck in the second minute, Johan Neeskens converting a penalty before a German had touched the ball. West Germany equalised in the 25th minute through a Paul Breitner penalty, and Gerd Muller scored the winner in the 43rd with a trademark close-range finish. The hosts held on in the second half to take the title.

## What the final meant

- West Germany's second World Cup title, twenty years after the Miracle of Bern.
- The victory of a pragmatic side over the Netherlands' celebrated Total Football.
- Five German players (Maier, Beckenbauer, Overath, Grabowski, Hottges) became the first to win World Cup gold, silver and bronze medals.
