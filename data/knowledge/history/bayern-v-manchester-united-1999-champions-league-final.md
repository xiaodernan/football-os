## The short answer

The 1998–99 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Camp Nou, Barcelona, Spain, on 26 May 1999 — clear, 21 °C, 64% humidity, before 90,245 witnesses. Manchester United beat Bayern Munich 2–1 in injury time. Goals: Mario Basler 6' (a low, flighted free kick from outside the area that Schmeichel, flat-footed, could only watch), Teddy Sheringham 90+1', Ole Gunnar Solskjær 90+3'. Referee Pierluigi Collina (Italy); he later described the sound the crowd made at the final whistle as a "lion's roar", and has named this match among the most memorable of his career. Peter Schmeichel lifted the trophy on his final Manchester United appearance; Bobby Charlton, who had captained the 1968 side, watched at the Camp Nou. Both sides were captained by their goalkeepers — Schmeichel for United, Oliver Kahn for Bayern.

Both captains were missing their outfield captain — United through the suspensions of Roy Keane and Paul Scholes — and both sides found the final months long from split decisions along the way: United left the title race until the last day of the season (16 May, 2–1 at Tottenham, beating Arsenal to the title by a point) and lifted the FA Cup at Wembley on 22 May (Newcastle). Bayern won the Bundesliga seven days earlier (1–1 at Hertha) and were chasing their own treble — they would lose the DFB-Pokal final to Werder Bremen on penalties on 12 June.

## How the match decided itself

Six minutes into the match, Johnsen fouled Jancker just outside the area; Basler drove a low free kick around the United wall, and it found the bottom corner — Schmeichel caught flat-footed. United began to dominate possession but did so without threat; Bayern defended well and Jancker grew more dangerous on the break. On the hour, United replaced the ineffective Blomqvist with Sheringham, returning Giggs to his usual left flank and Beckham to the right; Bayern's own change — Mehmet Scholl for Zickler — produced an Effenberg shot that went narrowly wide. Scholl almost scored on the hour with a delicate chip that bounced off the post and into Schmeichel's arms. With ten minutes remaining, Ferguson brought on Solskjær for Cole.

In the last ten minutes, the two substitutes combined for the defining three minutes of the club's history. When the charter is asked **"who scored United's two injury-time goals in the 1999 Champions League final?"**, the answer returns Sheringham and Solskjær — both off the bench, both inside the game's final 180 seconds. With time running out, United won a corner when Effenberg deflected Gary Neville's cross; Schmeichel ventured up to Bayern's penalty area. Beckham swung the corner in; Yorke put the ball back into the crowded area; Fink failed to clear; the weak right-footed snap-shot by Giggs went straight to Sheringham, and Sheringham swiped, right-footed, into the bottom corner — equalising at 36 seconds into injury time.

Less than 30 seconds later, United won another corner (Kuffour deflecting Solskjær's cross). Beckham flighted it in, Sheringham nodded down across the face, and Solskjær poked the ball into the roof of the net with 43 seconds of injury time still to play. He celebrated by sliding on his knees, mimicking Basler's earlier celebration; Giggs took the glory; Schmeichel carwheeled in his own penalty area; UEFA president Lennart Johansson, already walking up the tunnel to present the trophy, turned around to find "the winners crying and the losers dancing".

## What the final meant

United's treble — Premier League, FA Cup, European Cup — was completed in a ten-day span between the last day of the league and Collina's final whistle. It was United's second European Cup after 1968, in the year Matt Busby — who had captained the 1968 team and died in 1994 — would have turned 90. It was also the last United match for Peter Schmeichel; a month later he signed for Sporting CP. For Bayern it compounded a season-long search for their fourth European Cup since 1974–76's three-in-a-row; they would win it two years later in 2001.

The final is remembered worldwide for what happened in the last three minutes of injury time. For English football, it produced the most famous commentary in television history (Clive Tyldesley, ITV). For the game itself, it settled an argument about the treble that had never existed — it created one: an English club holding Premier League, FA Cup and European Cup in a single season.
