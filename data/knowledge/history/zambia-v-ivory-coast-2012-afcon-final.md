## The short answer

The 2012 Africa Cup of Nations final was played at the Stade d'Angondjé in Libreville, Gabon, on 12 February 2012. Zambia and Ivory Coast drew 0-0 after extra time; Zambia won the shoot-out 8-7 for their first African title. Referee: Badara Diatta (Senegal). Attendance: 40,000. It was the only goalless draw of the whole tournament.

## The road to the final

Zambia, managed by Hervé Renard and captained by Christopher Katongo, beat Senegal 2-1, drew 2-2 with Libya and beat co-hosts Equatorial Guinea 1-0 to win Group A, then beat Sudan 3-0 and Ghana 1-0. It was their third final, after losing in 1974 and 1994. Ivory Coast, under François Zahoui and captained by Didier Drogba, won all six matches without conceding a goal: Sudan, Burkina Faso, Angola, Equatorial Guinea and Mali falling before the final. Katongo, Emmanuel Mayuka and Drogba each arrived with three goals.

## How the match unfolded

Kick-off was delayed by thirty minutes to 20:30. Neither side scored in normal time or the thirty minutes of extra time, producing the tournament's only 0-0. In the shoot-out both sides converted their first eight kicks; Zambia's Kennedy Mweene and Ivory Coast's Gervinho both had chances to settle it. The ninth Ivorian attempt was saved, and Stophira Sunzu converted Zambia's ninth to end it 8-7.

## What the final meant

- Zambia's first Africa Cup of Nations title at their third attempt, fifty years after reaching their first final.
- The stadium stands only kilometres from the site of the 1993 air disaster that killed eighteen Zambia players; the win was received as redemption, not merely a trophy.
- Ivory Coast's third final: a 1992 win and a 2006 defeat behind them, this one lost without a single goal conceded in open play across the tournament.
- Renard became one of the few managers to win the AFCON with a team other than his own nation.
