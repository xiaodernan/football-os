## The short answer

The 2010–11 UEFA Champions League final was played on 28 May 2011 at Wembley Stadium in London, the rematch of the 2009 Rome final. Barcelona won 3–1 against Manchester United, goals by Pedro (27'), Lionel Messi (54') and David Villa (69'); Wayne Rooney equalised briefly (34'). The referee was Viktor Kassai (Hungary); attendance 87,695; kick-off 19:45 BST. This was Barcelona's fourth European Cup — 1992, 2006, 2009, 2011 — and it was played at the new Wembley's first European Cup final, with the stadium having hosted five European Cup finals in its old incarnation (1963, 1968, 1971, 1978, 1992).

## How the match was played

Pedro opened in the 27th minute. United replied within seven minutes through Wayne Rooney at 34'. The mid-game settled into a Barcelona tempo United could not maintain — Spain's champions comfortably control the ball, and United's route was the counter of the first half hour, not the last. In the 54th minute, Messi put Barcelona ahead again; in the 69th minute, Villa extended the result. United's shape could not re-equilibrate after Messi's second.

## What the route told us

Both finalists came in as domestic champions and group winners. Barcelona passed through the knockouts: Arsenal 4–3 on aggregate (1–2 away at Emirates, 3–1 home at Camp Nou), Shakhtar Donetsk 6–1 (5–1 home in front of its own fans, 1–0 away) and Real Madrid 3–1 (2–0 away at the Bernabéu, 1–1 home). Manchester United beat Marseille 2–1 (0–0 away, 2–1 home), defeated Chelsea 3–1 (1–0 away at Stamford Bridge, 2–1 home) and Schalke 04 6–1 (2–0 away at Veltins-Arena, 4–1 home). United were in a third final in four years; Barcelona were their second-time match in 2009 rematch.

## What the final meant

Barcelona won their fourth European Cup and confirmed the Guardiola era as the highest standard of the decade for club football — a rematch win in the same season as the domestic title. Messi took the UEFA Man of the Match (and the fans' MOTM) for his influence on the game. For Manchester United, the result marked two of their last three finals (2009, 2011) without lifting one. For Wembley, this was a second inauguration — the modern venue's first European Cup final, bringing the richest final in the stadium's history.
