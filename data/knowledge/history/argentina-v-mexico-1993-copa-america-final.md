## The short answer

The 1993 Copa America final was played at the Estadio Monumental in Guayaquil, Ecuador, on 4 July 1993. Argentina beat Mexico 2-1. Referee: Marcio Rezende (Brazil). Attendance: 40,000. Managed by Alfio Basile, Argentina won their fourteenth Copa America and defended the title they had won two years earlier.

## The road to the final

Argentina came through their group and the knockout rounds to reach another final, relying on the experience of a side that had won the trophy in 1991. Mexico, invited as a guest nation from outside CONMEBOL, progressed through the group stage and the knockout rounds to reach the showpiece match.

## How the match unfolded

Argentina controlled the final and won 2-1. Gabriel Batistuta scored both goals, taking his tally to a memorable height in a single tournament, and became the hero of the night. Mexico pulled one back but could not find an equaliser, and Argentina held on to lift the trophy again.

## What the final meant

- Argentina's fourteenth Copa America title and a successful defence of their 1991 crown.
- The first time a non-CONMEBOL nation reached a Copa America final, with Mexico's invited presence changing the competition's character.
- Argentina's last Copa America title until 2021, a wait of twenty-eight years.

## Match details

The final at the Estadio Monumental in Guayaquil on 4 July 1993 was refereed by Marcio Rezende of Brazil before 40,000 spectators. Argentina's victory was built on the firepower of Gabriel Batistuta, whose two goals settled the match and cemented his reputation as one of the tournament's great strikers. Mexico, the first non-CONMEBOL side to reach the final, had been invited to the competition and proved that guest nations could compete at the highest level. Alfio Basile's Argentina defended the title won in 1991, but this would be their last Copa America triumph for nearly three decades.
