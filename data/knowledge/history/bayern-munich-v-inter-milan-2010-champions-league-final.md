## The short answer

The 2009–10 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Santiago Bernabéu, Madrid, on 22 May 2010 at 20:45 — sunny, 25 °C, 32% humidity, before 73,490 witnesses. Bayern Munich 0–2 Inter Milan. Goals: Diego Milito 35' and 70'. Referee Howard Webb (England); assistants Mike Mullarkey and Darren Cann; fourth official Martin Atkinson. UEFA Man of the Match: Diego Milito; Fans' Man of the Match: Wesley Sneijder.

It was the first European Cup final ever held on a Saturday, and the first to be played between two group runners-up (Group A: Bordeaux first, Bayern second; Group F: Barcelona first, Inter second). Inter completed the treble — the first Italian club to do so — and their third European Cup: 45 years after their 1964 and 1965 wins under Helenio Herrera's Grande Inter.

## What the route told us

Bayern finished second in Group A (10 points — Bordeaux 16, Bayern 10, Juventus 8, Maccabi Haifa 0). Knockouts: Bayern finished second in Group A (10 points — Bordeaux 16, Bayern 10, Juventus 8, Maccabi Haifa 0) but won 4–4 on aggregate against both Fiorentina (2–1 home, 2–3 away) and Manchester United (2–1 home, 2–3 away) on the away-goals rule, and Lyon 4–0 on aggregate to reach the final. They had claimed the Bundesliga a fortnight earlier.

Inter finished second in Group F (9 points — Barcelona 11, Inter 9, Rubin Kazan 6, Dynamo Kyiv 5). Knockouts: Chelsea 3–1 on aggregate (2–1 at the San Siro, 1–0 at Stamford Bridge); CSKA Moscow 2–0 on aggregate (1–0 home, 1–0 away); Barcelona 3–2 on aggregate (3–1 home at the San Siro, 0–1 away at the Camp Nou).

## How the match decided itself

Inter's plan under Mourinho: defensive discipline, organized counters, a compact midfield, no midfielder leaked — Sneijder dictating transitions. Bayern took early control but could not convert. The opening goal came on 35 minutes: Júlio César launched long, Milito and Sneijder combined in a flick and return, and Milito finished low into the corner from near the penalty spot. Bayern threw attackers at the game — no breakthrough. Inter's second came in the 70th: Eto'o, on the break, played Milito, Milito took him past Van Buyten and struck inside the near post.

Inter kept it respectable: Schweinsteiger's long-range efforts went off target; Robben was unlucky with two free-kicks. At 63' Bayern replaced Altıntop with Klose; at 74' Olić with Mario Gómez; Inter closed it out.

## What the final meant

Inter's third European Cup — six years after Mourinho's Porto became 2004 winners, and thirteen years after Hitzfeld's Dortmund had broken the German spell (1997). With the win, Mourinho became only the third manager to win the European Cup with two clubs — after Happel (Feyenoord 1970, Hamburg 1983) and Hitzfeld (Dortmund 1997, Bayern 2001). Ten days later he was at Real Madrid.

Bayern extended the run: they lost another final two years later, 1–1 in Munich to Chelsea on penalties, then won in 2013 and 2020. For Inter it was the peak of a shelf that has never matched since — in 2023 and 2025 they would reach the final again and lose to treble-winning sides.

This match remains the benchmark for a perfectly executed defensive game plan and the most disciplined team performance of the era.
