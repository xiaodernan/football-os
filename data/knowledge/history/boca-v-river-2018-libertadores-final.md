## The short answer

The 2018 Copa Libertadores finals, known as the Superfinal, were contested by Boca Juniors and River Plate. The first leg finished 2-2 at La Bombonera on 11 November 2018; the second leg, moved to the Santiago Bernabéu in Madrid, finished 3-1 to River Plate after extra time on 9 December 2018. River Plate won 5-3 on aggregate for their fourth title.

## The road to the final

Boca Juniors finished runners-up in Group H and beat Libertad, Cruzeiro and Palmeiras in the knockout rounds. River Plate won Group D and eliminated Racing, Independiente and Grêmio. It was the first all-Argentine final and the first to feature two clubs from the same city — the Superclásico staged as a continental final.

## How the matches unfolded

In the first leg, Ábila put Boca ahead in the 34th minute, Lucas Pratto equalised a minute later, Benedetto restored Boca's lead before half-time, and an Izquierdoz own goal in the 61st minute made it 2-2. The second leg began with Benedetto giving Boca a 44th-minute lead. Pratto equalised in the 68th; the game went to extra time, where Wilmar Barrios was sent off. Juan Fernando Quintero put River ahead in the 109th minute, and Gonzalo Martínez sealed a 3-1 win into an empty net at 120+2.

## What the final meant

- River Plate's fourth Copa Libertadores title.
- The last Libertadores final played over two legs; from 2019 the final became a single match at a pre-selected venue.
- The second leg was the first — and, as of 2025, only — Copa Libertadores final leg played outside South America, staged at the Bernabéu after the attack on the Boca team bus forced CONMEBOL to move it.
- River qualified for the 2018 FIFA Club World Cup and the 2019 Recopa Sudamericana.

## Match details

The first leg at La Bombonera on 11 November 2018 was refereed by Roberto Tobar of Chile and watched by roughly 49,000 spectators (the published infobox records 54,000). It was postponed a day because of a waterlogged pitch. The second leg, originally due at River's Monumental on 24 November, was delayed after Boca's team bus was attacked with projectiles and pepper spray, and CONMEBOL eventually moved it to the Santiago Bernabéu in Madrid on 9 December. Real Madrid offered the stadium free of charge. Andrés Cunha of Uruguay refereed in front of 62,282. Benedetto scored first, Pratto equalised, and extra time produced Quintero's winner and Martínez's late empty-net strike after Barrios had been dismissed. The 5-3 aggregate gave River a fourth crown and closed the book on two-legged Libertadores finals.
