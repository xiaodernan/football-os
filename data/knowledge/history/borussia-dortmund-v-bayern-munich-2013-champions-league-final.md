## The short answer

The 2012–13 UEFA Champions League final was played at Wembley Stadium, London, on 25 May 2013 — sunny, 14 °C, 40% humidity, before 86,298 witnesses. Both finalists were German. Bayern Munich beat Borussia Dortmund 2–1 — Mario Mandžukić 60', İlkay Gündoğan 68' penalty, Arjen Robben 89'. Referee: Nicola Rizzoli (Italy); UEFA Man of the Match: Arjen Robben; Fans' Man of the Match: Manuel Neuer. Bayern's win made it their fifth European Cup, completing their first treble (third in history for Europe's clubs).

It was the first all-German final in the history of the European Cup or Champions League. Bayern came in from a 7–0 aggregate semi-final haul over FC Barcelona (4–0 at the Nou Camp, 3–0 at Wembley) — the heaviest total defeat in the Nou Camp's history to that point, and the last great evening of the Guardiola-era Bayern dynasty.

## What the route told us

Dortmund came through Group D as winners with 14 points (four wins, two draws). Knockouts: Shakhtar Donetsk 5–2 agg (2–2 away, 3–0 at home); Málaga 3–2 (0–0 away, 3–2 at home); Real Madrid 4–3 (4–1 at home at the Westfalenstadion, 2–0 away in Madrid). Bayern came through Group F with 13 points (first on goal difference over Valencia). Knockouts: Arsenal 3–3 agg (1–3 at the Emirates, 2–0 at home — won on the away-goals rule); Juventus 4–0 (2–0 at home, 2–0 in Turin); Barcelona 7–0 (4–0 at the Camp Nou, 3–0 at Wembley).

## How the match decided itself

Mandžukić opened Bayern's scoring in the 60th minute. Gündoğan equalised from the penalty spot in the 68th minute — awarded for a challenge on Mats Hummels. Robben's winner arrived in the 89th minute, the only one of the three goals to be a settled one-touch. The night, as the record shows, was decided to within ten minutes of the end of its result.

## What the final meant

It was Bayern's third consecutive all-German final against arch-rivals Dortmund and completed their treble. They also won the Bundesliga and DFB-Pokal in the same season. Manuel Neuer — Munich's keeper — had said nothing public beforehand about celebrating his clean sheet. In that same month, Dortmund announced that their homegrown attacking midfielder Mario Götze would move to the arch-rival next season for a reported €37M. Bayern went on to win the UEFA Super Cup against Chelsea; the Club World Cup, semi-final against Cape Town Spurs 2–0, and final against Atlético Mineiro.
