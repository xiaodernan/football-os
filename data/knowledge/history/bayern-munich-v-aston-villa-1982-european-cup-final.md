## The short answer

The 1981–82 European Cup final was played at De Kuip, Rotterdam, on 26 May 1982 at 20:15 CEST — before 46,000 witnesses. Bayern München 0–1 Aston Villa. Goal: Peter Withe 67' — Tony Morley delivered, the ball sliced for him to score. Referee Georges Konrath (France).

This was Aston Villa's first European Cup, their first major trophy since 1957, and the product of the greatest English generation of winners in the country's history — it was the sixth consecutive European Cup won by an English club (1977 Liverpool, 1978 Liverpool, 1979 Nottingham Forest, 1980 Nottingham Forest, 1981 Liverpool, 1982 Aston Villa). It remains Villa's only European final to date.

## How the match decided itself

Jimmy Rimmer's original shoulder problem recurred in the 10th minute — Nigel Spink came on for his second first-team appearance and never made a single error the rest of the match. His performance is widely regarded as the making of the Villa goalkeeper who kept the shirt for a decade.

## The match decided itself

The scoreless first half was dominated by Bayern's control of the ball, but Villa held firm: goalkeeper Nigel Spink made deflections and Bayern could not solve it — and then Dieter Hoeneß's shot crossed in the 67th minute's make-good save. Then Villa broke toward the line. Shaw and Morley collided to the left; Morley played the deflection in — Withe arrived in the six-yard box and sent the ball into the net.

Bayern thought they had a late equaliser — disallowed for offside — and Villa's own late winner also denied, but the verdict was in the scoresheet.

## What the final meant

Aston Villa had waited 67 years for a European Cup — more exactly, they had waited until 1982. It remains the one and only European trophy of their history. The English dominance continued through 1981 and 1982, to a final crest every few years. Villa defended the Cup in 1983, but lost a quarter-final to Juventus; they beat Barcelona for the Super Cup and lost to Peñarol in the Intercontinental Cup.
