## The short answer

The 1970 FIFA World Cup final was played at the Estadio Azteca in Mexico City on 21 June 1970. Brazil beat Italy 4-1. Goals: Pele 18' (header from a Rivelino cross), Gerson, Jairzinho and captain Carlos Alberto (Brazil); Roberto Boninsegna (Italy). Referee: Rudy Glockner (East Germany). Attendance: 107,412.

## The road to the final

Brazil won all three Group 3 matches, then beat Peru in the quarter-final and Uruguay in the semi-final. Italy topped Group 2 (one win, two draws), then beat hosts Mexico in the quarter-final and West Germany in a classic semi-final.

## How the match unfolded

Brazil struck first when Pele headed in a Rivelino cross in the 18th minute. Boninsegna equalised for Italy after a defensive blunder. In the second half Brazil's firepower overwhelmed Italy's cautious defensive system: Gerson fired in a powerful shot, Jairzinho headed in from a Pele knockdown, and captain Carlos Alberto finished the move widely regarded as the greatest goal in World Cup history - seven Brazilian players touched the ball before Pele laid it off for Alberto to smash home.

## What the final meant

- Brazil's third World Cup title, which allowed them to keep the Jules Rimet Trophy permanently.
- A Brazil side led by Pele, Tostao and Jairzinho often described as the greatest team ever assembled.
- Carlos Alberto's team goal became a byword for attacking football.
- Italys cautious catenaccio could not contain Brazils creativity.
