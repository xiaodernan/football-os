## The short answer

The 2018–19 UEFA Champions League final was played on 1 June 2019 at Estadio Metropolitano in Madrid. Liverpool won 2–0 — goals from Mohamed Salah inside the second minute and from Divock Origi in the 87th — under Jürgen Klopp and against Tottenham Hotspur, in the club's first European Cup final. It was the second all-English final in the competition's history after Manchester United–Chelsea in 2008, and the first final of the Champions League era to use the video assistant referee. Liverpool lifted the trophy; Tottenham remained one of the few English sides never to win it.

## How the match was played

Liverpool earned a penalty inside the first half-minute — Sissoko handled Mané's pass in the box — and Salah scored from the spot after 106 seconds of the match, making it the second-fastest goal in Champions League final history. Harry Kane alone carried any threat for Spurs, missing one headed effort when unmarked in the first half. In the 87th minute, substitute Divock Origi doubled the lead, finishing from a Shaqiri lay-off thirty metres out. Liverpool's combination of pressing and a defensive stability built on van Dijk (MOTM) held Spurs scoreless; their only attempt of the match was unthreatening from a distance.

## What the route told us

Group stage: Spurs finished second in Group B (8 points) behind Barcelona (14), Liverpool second in Group C (9) behind PSG (11). Round of 16: Spurs beat Dortmund 4–0 on aggregate; Liverpool beat Bayern Munich 3–1 on aggregate. Quarter-finals: Spurs and Manchester City traded 1–0 at home for Spurs, 3–4 away in Manchester; the 4–4 aggregate went to Spurs on away goals. Semi-finals: Spurs lost the first leg 1–0 to Ajax in Amsterdam, then won the second leg 3–2 at home with Lucas Moura's hat-trick — the aggregate 4–3 going their way in the 96th minute. Liverpool lost the first leg 3–0 at Camp Nou, then defeated the Catalan club 4–0 at Anfield to complete a 4–3 aggregate comeback.

## What the final meant

Klopp's first major trophy as Liverpool manager, the club's sixth European Cup, and their second of the Champions League era. Liverpool ranked as the third most-successful European Cup side up to that point (six titles) behind Real Madrid (13) and AC Milan (7). Tottenham, the only English side to reach a Champions League final and not win it, walked away devastated; for English football, the stage provided a second all-English final in the history of the competition, and a 2–0 scoreline whose shape owed more to early goals and disciplined defending than to the heavier investment patterns of the prior decade.
