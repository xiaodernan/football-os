## The short answer

The 2006 FIFA World Cup final was played at the Olympiastadion in Berlin on 9 July 2006. Italy and France drew 1-1 after extra time, and Italy won the penalty shoot-out 5-3 for their fourth World Cup title. Referee: Horacio Elizondo (Argentina). Attendance: 69,000. Andrea Pirlo was named man of the match.

## The road to the final

Italy, managed by Marcello Lippi and captained by Fabio Cannavaro, won Group E with two wins and a draw, then beat Australia 1-0, Ukraine 3-0 and hosts Germany 2-0 after extra time in the semi-final. France, under Raymond Domenech and captained by Zinedine Zidane, finished second in Group G, then beat Spain 3-1, defending champions Brazil 1-0 and Portugal 1-0. The final was the sixth World Cup final for Italy and the second for France; their last meeting at a major final was France's golden-goal win in the Euro 2000 final.

## How the match unfolded

In the 7th minute Elizondo awarded France a penalty when Marco Materazzi challenged Florent Malouda. Zidane converted it with a chipped Panenka that grazed the underside of the crossbar. Materazzi made amends in the 19th minute, heading Andrea Pirlo's corner past Fabien Barthez. Luca Toni hit the crossbar in the 36th minute; Gianluigi Buffon saved a Zidane header in the 104th. In the 108th minute Zidane headbutted Materazzi in the chest and was sent off in the 110th. The shoot-out went 5-3: David Trezeguet hit the crossbar for France, and Fabio Grosso converted the winning kick.

## What the final meant

- Italy's fourth World Cup crown, and first since 1982; they topped the world rankings in February 2007 for the first time since 1993.
- It was Zidane's last match. He was sent off in extra time yet was awarded the tournament's Golden Ball.
- Pirlo was man of the match; Buffon won the Golden Glove after conceding only one goal in the whole tournament, an own goal. Cannavaro won the 2006 Ballon d'Or.
- With eight Juventus players on the pitch, the final matched the 1934 record for players from one club.
