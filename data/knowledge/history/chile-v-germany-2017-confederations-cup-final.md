## The short answer

The 2017 FIFA Confederations Cup final was played at Krestovsky Stadium in Saint Petersburg, Russia, on 2 July 2017. Germany beat Chile 1-0. Referee: Milorad Mažić (Serbia). Attendance: 57,268. Marc-André ter Stegen was named man of the match.

## The road to the final

Chile, the Copa América holders, reached the final after a semi-final won on penalties against Portugal. Germany, the World Cup holders, reached the final with a 3-2 semi-final win over Mexico.

## How the match unfolded

Lars Stindl scored the only goal in the 20th minute, finishing a low cross from Timo Werner. Chile created chances but could not beat ter Stegen in the Germany goal, and the 1-0 scoreline held.

## What the final meant

- Germany's first and only FIFA Confederations Cup title.
- The tenth and final edition of the tournament — the last Confederations Cup match ever played.
- Ter Stegen, whose saves preserved the clean sheet, was named man of the match.

## Match details

The final at Krestovsky Stadium in Saint Petersburg on 2 July 2017 was refereed by Serbia's Milorad Mažić before 57,268 spectators. Germany, managed by Joachim Löw, struck in the 20th minute when Werner's low cross was finished by Stindl. Chile, the Copa América champions, pressed for an equaliser but were repeatedly denied by goalkeeper Marc-André ter Stegen, who was named man of the match. The 1-0 held to the whistle, giving Germany their first Confederations Cup — and, as the tournament was then discontinued, their last. It was the final match in the competition's history.
