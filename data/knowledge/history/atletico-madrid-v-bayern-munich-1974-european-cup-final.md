## The short answer

The 1973–74 European Cup final was played at the Heysel Stadium, Brussels, on 15 May 1974 at 20:15 CET, before 48,722 witnesses. Atlético Madrid 1–1 Bayern München after extra time — Luis Aragonés scored a goal in the 114th minute; Hans-Georg Schwarzenbeck equalised in the 120th. A replay followed two days later, on 17 May 1974, again at the Heysel, before roughly 23,000. Bayern won 4–0 — Uli Hoeneß 28', 82', Gerd Müller 56', 69'. Referee for the first final: Vital Loraux (Belgium); for the replay: Alfred Delcourt (Belgium).

This is the only European Cup final ever replayed. Bayern lifted their first of three consecutive European Cups.

## How the match decided itself

Atlético Madrid had pressed throughout — Aragonés' 114th-minute goal put them on the path. With six minutes remaining Schwarzenbeck's wallop equalised — a last-minute goal of the decade. Neither side could find a winner in extra time; the competition regulations demanded a replay, and two days later Bayern beat Atlético 4–0 — Hoeneß and Müller did not miss, two apiece.

## What the final meant

Bayern opened their three-year European dynasty (1974, 1975, 1976). Beckenbauer captained West Germany to the World Cup in July 1974 — inside the same summer, his first season with the German flag on the shoulder. Atlético Madrid became the fallen giants of the decade — they had reached the Copa del Rey final in 1972, and five of their starters also contested the World Cup final the same summer.
