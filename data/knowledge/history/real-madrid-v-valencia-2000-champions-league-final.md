## The short answer

The 1999–2000 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Stade de France, Saint-Denis, France, on 24 May 2000 at 20:45 — before 80,000 witnesses. It was the first European Cup or Champions League final to feature two clubs from the same country. Real Madrid 3–0 Valencia. Goals: Fernando Morientes 39' (header), Steve McManaman 67' (volley), Raúl 75' (breakaway). Referee Stefano Braschi (Italy). The UEFA Man of the Match was not definitively recorded in the public summary I used for this chapter.

Real Madrid's eighth European Cup — their second in three years — and Vicente del Bosque's first title in charge of the club.

## What the route told us

Real Madrid: Group E winners on 13 points (Porto 12, Olympiacos 7, Molde 3), then second in Group C behind Bayern Munich (Bayern 13, Real Madrid 10, Dynamo Kyiv 10). Knockouts: Manchester United 3–2 on aggregate (0–0 at the Bernabéu first leg, 3–2 away win at Old Trafford — Manchester United were the defending champions); Bayern Munich 3–2 on aggregate (2–0 home first leg, 1–2 away second leg).

Valencia: Group F winners on 12 points (Bayern 9, Rangers 7, PSV 4). Knockouts: Lazio 5–3 on aggregate (5–2 at the Mestalla, 0–1 at the Stadio Olimpico); Barcelona 5–3 on aggregate (4–1 at the Mestalla, 1–2 at the Camp Nou).

## How the match decided itself

Morientes headed the opener in the 39th minute; McManaman doubled the lead with a superb 67th-minute volley — the Liverpool striker's defining moment at a foreign club, the first English player to win the European Cup with a non-English side; Raúl made it 3–0 on 75 minutes, gathering the half-cleared ball through the middle and beating Santiago Cañizares after he had been released around the halfway line.

Valencia — first ever European final — came in off their two most emphatic home knockout wins of the season; they never produced a single shot on target in the final.

## What the final meant

It was Real Madrid's eighth European Cup — their second in three years, and Vicente del Bosque's first trophy in charge. McManaman became the first English player to win the European Cup with a non-English club. The summer followed with the arrival of Luis Figo — the first buy of what became the galáctico strategy.

Real Madrid also lifted the 2000 UEFA Super Cup on aggregate against Galatasaray in August that summer.
