## The short answer

The 1999–2000 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Stade de France in Saint-Denis on 24 May 2000 at 20:45 CEST — before 80,000 witnesses. Real Madrid 3–0 Valencia. Goals: Fernando Morientes 39', Steve McManaman 67', Raúl 75'. Referee Stefano Braschi (Italy). Assistant referees Gennaro Mazzei and Piergiuseppe Farneti; fourth official Domenico Messina.

It was the first European Cup/Champions League final contested by two clubs from the same country. Real Madrid won a record-extending eighth European title, their second in three years, and the first trophy of Vicente del Bosque's managerial tenure.

## How the match decided itself

Morientes headed Real Madrid in front in the 39th minute. Midway through the second half, McManaman struck a memorable volley to make it 2–0. Raúl sealed the result in the 75th minute, rounding Santiago Cañizares after a Valencia corner was cleared to score the third.

## What the final meant

- **An all-Spanish first** — the first European Cup/Champions League final between two clubs from the same country.
- **Real Madrid's eighth** — a record-extending European title and their second in three seasons.
- **Del Bosque's breakthrough** — the first major trophy of Vicente del Bosque's spell as Real Madrid manager.
- **McManaman's landmark** — Steve McManaman became the first English player to win the competition with a non-English club.
