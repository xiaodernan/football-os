## The short answer

The 1966–67 European Cup final was played at the Estádio Nacional, Lisbon, on 25 May 1967 at 17:30 WEST — before 45,000 witnesses. Celtic 2–1 Inter Milan. Goals: Sandro Mazzola 7' (pen.) for Inter; Tommy Gemmell 63', Stevie Chalmers 84' for Celtic. Referee Kurt Tschenscher (West Germany).

It was Celtic's first European final; Inter were defending champions (twice in the previous three years). Celtic became the first British club — and the first from northern Europe — to win the European Cup. All Celtic players were born within a 30-mile radius of Glasgow. They became known as the Lisbon Lions.

## What the route told us

Celtic: Scottish Division One champions (their 21st title). First round: Zürich 5–0 agg (2–0 H, 3–0 A); Second round: Nantes 6–2 (3–1 A, 3–1 H); Quarter-finals: Vojvodina 2–1 agg (0–1 A, 2–0 H); Semi-finals: Dukla Prague 3–1 agg (3–1 H, 0–0 A).

Inter: 1965–66 Serie A champions (their tenth title). First round: Torpedo Moscow 1–0 agg (1–0 H, 0–0 A); Second round: Vasas 4–1 agg (2–1 H, 2–0 A); Quarter-finals: Real Madrid 3–0 agg (1–0 H, 2–0 A); Semi-finals: CSKA Sofia 2–2 agg, then a deciding play-off in Bologna 1–0 (Cappellini), moved to Italy after a gate-money arrangement.

The turning point before the final: Luis Suárez (Inter's captain, the world's best midfielder, Ballon d'Or 1960) missed the match with a knee injury, and Jair was also out. He was replaced by Mauro Bicicli — and paid for it.

## How the match decided itself

Mazzola took a seventh-minute penalty after Jim Craig was ruled to have fouled Cappellini in the box. Inter fell back on Catenaccio — deep, layered, densely held shape — and shipped nine men in defence for the rest of the match. Celtic attacked. Auld hit the bar; Johnstone header tipped over by Sarti. Gemmell finally broke through with a 63rd-minute strike from outside the box after Jim Craig's pass. Five minutes from the end, Chalmers bundled Bobby Murdoch's long shot beyond the wrong-footed Sarti. Celtic delivered.

## What the final meant

This result ended La Grande Inter — Inter never recovered from losing the Scudetto, the Coppa Italia (semi-finals) and the European Cup in that season; Helenio Herrera moved on the following year. Jock Stein's Celtic opened a decade as Britain's benchmark — Lisbon '67, the double in 1968 (League and Cup), the 1969 League Cup and the 1967 European Cup. Celtic's next final was 1970 in Milan (lost 2–1 after extra time to Feyenoord).
