## The short answer

The 2016–17 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Millennium Stadium in Cardiff, Wales, on 3 June 2017 at 19:45 local time under a closed retractable roof — the first European Cup final played indoors. Real Madrid beat Juventus 4–1 to claim their twelfth European Cup, the first successful title defence since AC Milan in 1990. Goalscorers: Cristiano Ronaldo 20' (deflected off Leonardo Bonucci, opened the scoring) and 64' (cut-back from Luka Modrić for his second), Casemiro 61' (deflected off Sami Khedira), Marco Asensio 90' (Marcelo's cut-back from the left). Mario Mandžukić equalised for Juventus at 27'. Referee Felix Brych (Germany); Player of the match: Cristiano Ronaldo. Attendance 65,842. It was a repeat of the 1998 final, Madrid winning 1–0.

## How the match was played

Juventus were the better side in the opening 20 minutes — Higuaín had two shots saved simply by Keylor Navas; Miralem Pjanić's more dangerous chance was pushed wide by Navas — but Real Madrid scored first: Dani Carvajal one-twoed with Ronaldo on the right and squared, Ronaldo shot low to the left corner, and the ball deflected in off Bonucci's right foot. Seven minutes later, Mandžukić chested a ball from Higuaín down and hooked it over his shoulder over his own marker and over Navas's head — an equaliser that was immediately compared to Zinedine Zidane's 2002 finish. Real Madrid were overwhelmed defensively in the first half, Sergio Ramos and Carvajal both picked up bookings, but came out stronger in the second half: Casemiro struck from distance in the 61st minute, deflected off Khedira, and three minutes later Ronaldo received Modrić's cut-back on the right edge of the six-yard box to finish for his second of the night and his second straight final with a brace. Juventus picked up three further yellow cards, including one to the newly-introduced Juan Cuadrado, who was sent off in the 84th minute for a shove on Ramos. Asensio — the third of Real Madrid's substitutes — pulled Marcelo's cut-back back across the face in the 90th minute to settle it 4–1.

## What the route told us

Juventus had not lost across the entire season — Group H winners on 14 points (Sevilla 0–0, Dinamo Zagreb 4–0, Lyon 1–0, Lyon 1–1, Sevilla 3–1 away, Dinamo Zagreb 2–0 home), conceding only three goals across all competitions in this CL, eliminating Porto 3–0 in the round of 16 (2–0 away, 1–0 home), demolishing Barcelona 3–0 on aggregate in the quarter-final (3–0 at home; 0–0 away), and seeing off Monaco 4–1 on aggregate in the semi-final (2–0 away, 2–1 at home). Juventus had won the 2016–17 Serie A and the 2016–17 Coppa Italia in the days leading into the final.

Real Madrid finished second in Group F behind Borussia Dortmund on 12 points, conceded nothing more than necessary in neutral venues (two 2–2 draws against Dortmund), and scored 32 goals across the campaign — the season's best. Knockout stage: Napoli 6–2 on aggregate (3–1 at the San Paolo; 3–1 at the Santiago Bernabéu), Bayern Munich 6–3 on aggregate in the quarter-finals (2–1 away, 4–2 a.e.t. at home), and local rivals Atlético Madrid 4–2 in the semi-finals (3–0 at home, 1–2 away — the fourth straight season in which Real Madrid had eliminated Atlético in the Champions League).

## What the final meant

For Real Madrid it was a double — La Liga secured on the final day of the season on 21 May — a fourth consecutive European Cup completed two seasons later, an unmatched club record of twelve titles, and the first successful title defence since Milan in 1990. For Juventus it was a ninth final and seventh lost (wins at Heysel 1985 and Rome 1996); the 2015 final against Barcelona is the direct precursor loss that still stings. For the competition: the 62nd season of Europe's top flight, 25 since the Champions League renaming; the first Champions League final played under a closed roof, representing the first indoor European Cup decision. For Cardiff, it was the city's first and only Champions League final to date — a sold-out 65,842 closed inside with the roof down to hold the night.
