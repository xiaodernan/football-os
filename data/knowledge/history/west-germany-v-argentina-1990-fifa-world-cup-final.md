## The short answer

The 1990 FIFA World Cup final was played at the Stadio Olimpico in Rome, Italy, on 8 July 1990. West Germany beat Argentina 1-0, with a late penalty from Andreas Brehme the only goal. Referee: Edgardo Codesal (Mexico). Attendance: 73,603.

## The road to the final

West Germany topped Group D and beat the Netherlands, Czechoslovakia and England. Argentina advanced from their group and beat Brazil, England (on penalties) and Italy (on penalties).

## How the match unfolded

A cagey final produced few chances. Argentina's defensive, physical approach frustrated West Germany, and the game seemed headed for extra time until a late penalty was awarded. Andreas Brehme converted it low to the right, securing a 1-0 win. It was the last match of West Germany as a nation before reunification.

## What the final meant

- West Germany's third World Cup title, their last before Germany reunified later in 1990.
- The first final rematch — a reversal of 1986, when Argentina beat West Germany.
- Argentina became the first defending champion to lose a final and the first team not to score in one.
- West Germany became the first side to reach three consecutive finals (1982, 1986, 1990).
