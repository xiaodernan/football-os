## The short answer

The 2013–14 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Estádio da Luz, Lisbon, Portugal, on 24 May 2014 at 19:45 — partly cloudy, 17 °C, 51% humidity, before 60,976 witnesses. Real Madrid 4–1 Atlético Madrid after extra time. Goals: Diego Godín 36', Sergio Ramos 90+3', Gareth Bale 110', Marcelo 118', Cristiano Ronaldo 120' (penalty). Referee Björn Kuipers (Netherlands); fourth official Cüneyt Çakır. UEFA Man of the Match: Ángel Di María; Fans' Man of the Match: Sergio Ramos.

It was the first final in European Cup history between two teams from the same city — and the first between two Madrid clubs. It secured Real Madrid's record tenth European Cup — La Décima — 12 years after their ninth, in Lisbon, watched by more than 60,000, and it triggered scenes that Spanish sport still talks about as the defining late-equaliser of the century.

## What the route told us

Real Madrid: Group B winners (16 points — Galatasaray 7, Juventus 6, Copenhagen 4), Schalke 9–2 on aggregate (6–1 away, 3–1 home), Borussia Dortmund 3–2 on aggregate (3–0 home, 0–2 away) and the semi-final against defending European champions Bayern Munich: 5–0 on aggregate (1–0 home, 4–0 at the Allianz Arena).

Atlético Madrid, in their first La Liga title season since 1996 (won a week earlier), topped Group G (16 points — Zenit 6, Porto 5, Austria Wien 5) and beat Milan 5–1 on aggregate (1–0 away, 4–1 home); Barcelona 2–1 on aggregate (1–1 away at the Camp Nou, 1–0 home); Chelsea 3–1 on aggregate (0–0 home, 3–1 away at Stamford Bridge).

## How the match decided itself

Real Madrid started without Xabi Alonso (suspended) and Pepe (rested); Raphaël Varane, 21 at the time, partnered Sergio Ramos in central defence. Atlético's Diego Costa came off injured inside eight minutes — the story around him all week: bench-warming since April, he played anyway, on a hamstring, widely reported as "horse-placenta treatment" the day before.

Atlético took the lead at 36 minutes: Juanfran headed a corner back across the face of goal, and Diego Godín volleyed it over Iker Casillas from close range. An entire second half of attacks yielded only frustration until the third minute of stoppage time, when Sergio Ramos levelled for 1–1 from a right-side Luka Modrić corner.

Extra time: Ángel Di María went past three, Courtois could not clear, Bale headed the rebound in from two yards eight minutes after the start of the second period of extra time (110'); Marcelo 118', left foot, left inside the penalty area; Ronaldo in the 120th minute converted a penalty after he was fouled by Gabi — his 17th goal in the tournament, the record. Simeone ran onto the pitch at 120+3' to get in Varane's face after the Frenchman kicked the ball at him in celebration; Simeone was sent off; Varane was booked.

## What the final meant

It was Real Madrid's tenth European Cup — La Décima — and their first European crown since 2002. Carlo Ancelotti (2003, 2007, 2014) joined Bob Paisley as the second manager to win three European Cups as a boss (Paisley: 1977, 1978, 1981); he added a fourth with Madrid in 2022, the first to take four. It was Atlético's second final, 40 years after their 1974 tie against Bayern Munich (that tie ended 1–1 and was replayed in Brussels two days later; Bayern won 4–0). They had won La Liga a week earlier — a first since 1996 — and entered knowing this final was a chance at an unforgettable double. They went ahead within the half and held on until the last kick of regulation before conceding the goal that will be remembered at the Estádio da Luz forever. Simeone fell short in his first European Cup final; he would return in 2016 and lose again.

BBC sports desk's line was its own headline. This match is regarded as one of the greatest finals in history.

## The wider meaning

- Lisbon entered European Cup final history for the second time (1967: Celtic 2–1 Inter).
- The new Estádio da Luz (built 2003, capacity 65,000) hosted its first European Cup final.
- It was the seventh final to go to extra time; the first decided in extra time without penalties.
- The second highest-scoring final in Champions League era after the 3–3 Milan–Liverpool (2005).
- Cristiano Ronaldo left as the tournament's top scorer with 17 goals; he became one of the few players to score and assist in a CL final.
