## The short answer

The 1930 FIFA World Cup final - the first World Cup final ever played - was held at the Estadio Centenario in Montevideo, Uruguay, on 30 July 1930. Hosts Uruguay beat Argentina 4-2. Goals: Pablo Dorado 12', Pedro Cea 57', Santos Iriarte 68', Hector Castro 89' (Uruguay); Carlos Peucelle 20', Guillermo Stabile 37' (Argentina). Referee: John Langenus (Belgium). Attendance: 68,346.

## The road to the final

Uruguay won Group 3 (1-0 Peru, 4-0 Romania) and beat Yugoslavia 6-1 in the semi-final. Argentina won Group 1 (1-0 France, 6-3 Mexico, 3-1 Chile) and beat the United States 6-1 in the other semi-final.

## How the match unfolded

Uruguay struck first through Dorado in the 12th minute. Argentina equalised through Peucelle on 20 minutes and turned the game around when tournament top scorer Stabile made it 2-1 just before half-time. Uruguay levelled through Cea on 57 minutes and grabbed the lead for good through Iriarte on 68 minutes. Castro sealed the 4-2 win a minute from time.

## What the final meant

- The first World Cup title, won by the host nation.
- Uruguay manager Alberto Suppici, aged 31, remains the youngest coach to win a World Cup.
- The final was the first to feature two Spanish-speaking nations.
- FIFA president Jules Rimet presented the trophy that later bore his name.
