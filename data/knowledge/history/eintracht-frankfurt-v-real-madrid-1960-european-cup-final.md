## The short answer

The 1959–60 European Cup final was played at Hampden Park, Glasgow, Scotland, on 18 May 1960 at 19:30 BST — before 127,621 witnesses, then the largest attendance ever for a European Cup final, and still one of the biggest in the competition's history — and the first European Cup final held in the United Kingdom. Eintracht Frankfurt 3–7 Real Madrid. Goals: Richard Kress 18', Erwin Stein 72', 75' for Frankfurt; Alfredo Di Stéfano 27', 30', 73' and Ferenc Puskás 45+1', 56' (penalty), 60', 71' — both hat-tricks — for Real Madrid. Referee Jack Mowat (Scotland).

It was Real Madrid's fifth consecutive European Cup (1956–1960), the only club to win the competition in its first five editions. Frankfurt led the first exchanges; Madrid turned the score around by the death of the first half; the second half turned into a procession. The final set the competition record for most goals in a final (still standing at 10) and equalled the record for largest winning margin (broken in 1994 by Milan 4–0 and again by PSG 5–0 in 2025).

## What the route told us

Eintracht Frankfurt: German champions 1959 (the club's only title). First round: Young Boys 5–2 on aggregate (4–1 away first leg, 1–1 home second leg). Quarter-finals: Wiener Sport-Club 3–2 on aggregate (2–1 home, 1–1 away). Semi-finals: Rangers 12–4 on aggregate (6–1 home in the first leg, 6–3 away at Ibrox). Frankfurt had threatened to walk away from the competition because DFB had banned any German club from European competition against Real Madrid with Puskás in the side — the FA required Puskás to make a formal written apology before Frankfurt's participation could proceed; he did, and the final proceeded.

Real Madrid: Group/preliminary round drawn from the defending champions. First round: Jeunesse Esch 12–2 on aggregate (7–0 home, 5–2 away). Quarter-finals: Nice 6–3 on aggregate (2–3 away loss first leg, 4–0 home second leg). Semi-finals: Barcelona 6–2 on aggregate (3–1 home first leg, 3–1 away at the Camp Nou in the return). Madrid entered the final as the four-time defending champions — the only team to have won the competition.

## How the match decided itself

Frankfurt opened the scoring: Kress 18' — Frankfurt led. Madrid led themselves back with two Di Stéfano goals by the half hour (27', 30'). Puskás scored in stoppage time of the first half, on 45+1'. From there the dam broke: Puskás made it three more in the second half (56' penalty, 60', 71'). Stein struck twice for Frankfurt (72', 75') to give the score its final shape; Di Stéfano completed his hat-trick in between them with the 73rd-minute third.

## What the final meant

- Real Madrid completed their fifth consecutive European Cup (1956, 1957, 1958, 1959 and 1960); they were the only winners of the first five editions and hold the competition record at 15 titles.
- The match set both the single-match scoring benchmark (10 goals — still standing) and the record for the largest winning margin until PSG beat Inter 5–0 in the 2025 final (Milan had equalled the Madrid 4-goal margin in 1994 4–0).
- Around 70 million across Europe watched on television. This was the first European Cup final in the United Kingdom.

Paco Gento's dribbling powered Madrid's left; Santamaría held the back; Canário — the Brazilian winger — fed the inside forwards; Di Stéfano and Puskás created the scoring. Frankfurt's Kress was brave but outclassed within minutes.
