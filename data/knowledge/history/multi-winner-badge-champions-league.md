## The short answer

UEFA keeps a permanent badge — the multi-winner badge — for every club that wins the European Cup/Champions League at least five times total, or three consecutive times. Six clubs hold it: Real Madrid, Ajax, Bayern Munich, AC Milan, Liverpool and Barcelona. The badge is not a trophy; it is a piece of consecutive and total history that cannot be re-earned, only displayed.

## Who holds it and how

- Real Madrid — 15 titles (1956, 1957, 1958, 1959, 1960, 1966, 1998, 2000, 2002, 2014, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2022, 2024). The first back-to-back-to-back-to-back-to-back, 1956–60, more titles than anywhere else on earth, and twenty of the total Spanish-won finals between Real Madrid and Barcelona (15 + 5).
- AC Milan — seven titles (1963, 1969, 1989, 1990, 1994, 2003, 2007).
- Liverpool — six titles (1977, 1978, 1981, 1984, 2005, 2019).
- Bayern Munich — six titles (1974, 1975, 1976, 2001, 2013, 2020), with the 1974–76 sweep of three on the bounce.
- Barcelona — five titles (1992, 2006, 2009, 2011, 2015).
- Ajax — four titles including the 1971–73 three-in-a-row, which is why the badge exists in that form at all.

## What the badge represents

The badge is printed to be read from the dressing room, not the trophy cabinet: it signals that a club has built formations that survive decade changes, and that tactics recur across generations — Cruyff's Ajax, Dalglish's Liverpool, Heynckes's Bayern, Flick's Bayern, Ancelottis's Madrid whites. The badge cannot be bought; it cannot be qualified for; it cannot be traded in an academy; it accumulates over decades and stands as the one European distinction that is earned only by repetition.

## Where the badge stops mattering

No amount of badges mitigates a missing Sunday match licence. The badge says a club has done it; it cannot substitute for a mediocre season, a bad scouting window, or an overpaid midfield trio. The badge is recognition, not healing. It is the closest club football has to an aristocracy — but what it means is precision repeated, not money spent.
