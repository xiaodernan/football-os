## The short answer

The 1974–75 European Cup final was played at the Parc des Princes, Paris, on 28 May 1975 at 19:15 CET before 48,374 witnesses. Bayern München 2–0 Leeds United. Goals: Franz Roth 71', Gerd Müller 81'. Referee Michel Kitabdjian (France).

This was Bayern's second consecutive European title — and the end of Leeds' European Cup dream. Bayern sat 10th in the Bundesliga that season, still a European Cup winner; Leeds trailed in 9th in the First Division, on a downfall season whose climax was a night at the Parc des Princes — evocative, controversial, and the reason Leeds' European record for a generation was sealed.

Leeds United: First round: Zürich 5–3 agg (4–1 H, 1–2 A); Second round: Újpesti Dózsa 5–1 agg (2–1 A, 3–0 H); Quarter-finals: Anderlecht 4–0 agg (3–0 H, 1–0 A); Semi-finals: FC Barcelona 3–2 agg (2–1 H, 1–1 A).

## How the match decided itself

The opening was feral. Leeds had a physical edge; two Leeds players appealed for penalties that Kitabdjian refused; Bayern's injuries began immediately — Björn Andersson broke his leg in a crunch by Terry Yorath in the 4th minute, the "most brutal foul I think I have ever seen" (Uli Hoeneß). Uli Hoeneß himself tore a knee ligament in the 37th.

In the 62nd minute, Billy Bremner was denied from five metres by Sepp Maier's reflex save; less than a minute later, a goal by Peter Lorimer was disallowed — Bremner was judged to be in a passive offside position at the six-yard box, after the linesman (who had originally retreated to halfway) was summoned by Beckenbauer. Riots broke out among Leeds' travelling contingent almost immediately afterward; the match never calmed.

Bayern counter-attacked twice against the torrent: Franz Roth opened the score in the 71st (a Torstensson feed), and Müller clinched it in the 81st after Kapellmann's cross.

## What the final meant

This was Bayern's second of three consecutive European Cups (1974, 1975, 1976). Dettmar Cramer took home his first of three international titles managing Bayern. Leeds never reached a final again for 21 years — UEFA banned them from Europe for four years (reduced to two), and the climb back was long.
