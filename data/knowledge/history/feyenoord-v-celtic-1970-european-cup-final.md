## The short answer

The 1969–70 European Cup final was played at the San Siro, Milan, on 6 May 1970, before 53,187 witnesses. Feyenoord 2–1 Celtic after extra time. Goals: Tommy Gemmell 30' for Celtic; Rinus Israël 32', and Ove Kindvall 117' for Feyenoord. Referee Concetto Lo Bello (Italy) — same referee as the 1968 Wembley final.

It was Feyenoord's and the Netherlands' first European Cup — the Austrian Ernst Happel became the first European Cup-winning coach to later win the trophy with a second club (Hamburg SV, 1983). Celtic's loss ended the Lisbon Lions' era, three years after their 1967 title.

Celtic: First round: Basel 2–0 agg (0–0 A, 2–0 H); Second round: Benfica 3–3 on aggregate (3–0 H, 0–3 a.e.t. away — the tie's deeper measure lay in the extra-time second leg at the Luz); Quarter-finals: Fiorentina 3–1 agg (3–0 H, 0–1 A); Semi-finals: Leeds United 3–1 agg (1–0 A, 2–1 H).

## How the match decided itself

Gemmell gave Celtic the lead on 30 minutes — his second European Cup final goal against a Dutch club in three years.

Israël's head equalised two minutes later in the 32nd — remarkably, the first goal Feyenoord scored in this European campaign away from home — though nominally, the San Siro was Milan's home, neutral to both clubs in the 1970 final. Happel's double-marking of Jimmy Johnstone and the iron midfield of Franz Hasil, Willem van Hanegem and Wim Jansen was the tactical heart of the result.

The second half offered stalemate: Celtic pressed, Feyenoord countered; the goal line was crossed zero times until the last seconds of extra time. With barely any time left, a long ball was sent into the penalty area, Celtic captain Billy McNeill misjudged it (the confusion of the play suggesting a penalty would be called); Kindvall came on and, before the referee could whistle, chipped the advancing  goalkeeper Evan Williams off the spot and into the net (117') — the winning goal. Celtic lost 2–1.

Happel's tactical read, and the midfield which resulted, is the through-line: Hasil, van Hanegem, Jansen > the Scots.

## What the final meant

This was the Netherlands' first European Cup. Feyenoord followed it with the 1970 Intercontinental Cup — Kindvall scored twice in the decisive play-off in Rotterdam, beating Estudiantes of Argentina. Theirs remains the only Dutch European Cup.

Celtic's loss was the end of the Lisbon Lions era — their second final appearance. Billy McNeill had played both Club finals (1967 W, 1970 L), a captain of remarkable consistency.
