## The short answer

The 2015–16 UEFA Champions League final was played at Stadio San Siro (Stadio Giuseppe Meazza) in Milan, Italy, on 28 May 2016 at 20:45 CEST — cloudy, 27 °C, 45% humidity — in front of 71,942 witnesses. It was the sixth European Cup final to feature two teams from the same association, the third all-Spanish final, the second between two clubs from the same city, and the rematch of the 2014 final two years earlier. Real Madrid beat Atlético Madrid 1–1 after extra time and 5–3 on penalties to lift their record 11th European Cup — their first under Zinedine Zidane, the winning scorer of the 2002 final at Glasgow. Goals: Sergio Ramos 15' (a Toni Kroos free-kick flicked on by Gareth Bale, Ramos poking past Oblak — Clattenburg knew at half-time the move had contained an offside; the goal stood), Yannick Carrasco 79' (Juanfran's cross from the right, close range). Referee Mark Clattenburg (England); Man of the match Sergio Ramos. The shoot-out: Vázquez, Marcelo, Bale, Ramos, Ronaldo all scored for Madrid; Griezmann, Gabi, Saúl scored for Atlético; Juanfran missed the fourth kick; Real won it 5–3.

## What the route told us

Real Madrid came through Group A as winners — Shakhtar Donetsk 4–0 (H) and 4–3 (A), Malmö FF 8–0 (H) and 2–0 (A), Paris Saint-Germain 0–0 (A) and 1–0 (H). In the knockouts: Roma 4–0 on aggregate (2–0 away, 2–0 home); VfL Wolfsburg 3–2 on aggregate (a 0–2 loss away at the Volkswagen Arena, then 3–0 at the Bernabéu); Manchester City 1–0 on aggregate (0–0 away, 1–0 home). Atlético Madrid came through Group C as winners — Galatasaray 2–0 (A and H), Astana 4–0 (H) and 0–0 (A), Benfica 2–1 (A) and a 1–2 loss (H). In the knockouts: PSV Eindhoven 0–0 on aggregate, won 8–7 on penalties; Barcelona 3–2 on aggregate (1–2 defeat away at the Camp Nou, 2–0 at home at the Vicente Calderón); Bayern Munich 2–2 on away goals (1–0 home, 1–2 away at the Allianz Arena).

## What the final meant

For Real Madrid, it was a record 11th European Cup — La Undécima — in a 14th final (their 18th in all UEFA club competitions). Zidane, aiming at becoming the seventh man to win the competition as both player and manager, had taken over from Rafael Benítez in January after a disastrous first half of the season; Real had been left out of La Liga contention by then (Barcelona retained the league on 91 points, Real 90, Atlético 88).

For Atlético Madrid, it was a third European Cup final — after 1974, when the only replay in the competition's history tilted against them (1–1 at the Heysel, then a 4–0 loss three days later), and 2014 (1–1, lost 4–1 on penalties, also to Real Madrid). All three ended in defeat, and the San Siro night stretched their pursuit of a first European Cup into another decade of summer pain.

For the tournament, it guaranteed the city of Madrid becoming the most successful city in the European Cup — 11 wins and 17 final appearances, overtaking Milan. It also marked the first time a Champions League final had been staged at San Siro with neither tenant club involved: AC Milan and Internazionale had both failed to qualify for any European competition that season. Alicia Keys opened the show — the first live music performance at a Champions League final.
