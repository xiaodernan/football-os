## The short answer

The 1970–71 European Cup final was played at Wembley Stadium, London, on 2 June 1971. Ajax 2–0 Panathinaikos. Goals: Dick van Dijk 5', Arie Haan 87'. Referee Jack Taylor (England). Attendance 83,179.

This was Panathinaikos' first and only European Cup final — the only time in history a Greek club reached one. It was Ajax's first European Cup — under Rinus Michels, in an England stadium, two goals down in trajectory to their future trilogy (1971, 1972, 1973).

## What the route told us

Ajax: First round: 17 Nëntori Tirana 4–2 agg (2–2 A, 2–0 H); Second round: Basel 5–1 agg (3–0 H, 2–1 A); Quarter-finals: holders Celtic 3–1 agg (3–0 H, 0–1 A); Semi-finals: Atlético Madrid 3–1 agg (0–1 A, 3–0 H).

Panathinaikos: a four-round first-time finalist. First round: Jeunesse Esch 7–1 agg (2–1 A, 5–0 H); Second round: Slovan Bratislava 4–2 agg (3–0 H, 1–2 A); Quarter-finals: Everton 1–1 agg (1–1 A, 0–0 H — advancing on away goals); Semi-finals: Crvena zvezda 4–4 agg (1–4 A, 3–0 H — advancing on away goals at home in Thessaloniki after losing the first leg in Belgrade).

## How the match decided itself

Van Dijk opened the scoring in the fifth minute — a classic of that early-streak era.

Panathinaikos pressed, but never found an equaliser; in the 87th minute, Arie Haan's effort deflected off Anthimos Kapsis and into the net. Ajax held on for their first European Cup.

## What the final meant

Michels won this one final (1971); the 1972 and 1973 finals were under his successor Ștefan Kovács. Rinus Michels had built Total Football on a production line — Cruyff, Vasović, Suurbier, Stuy, Neeskens, Mühren, Keizer, Blankenburg.

For Panathinaikos, this final remains their only European final to date; Puskás's side beat Everton and Crvena zvezda on away goals en route to Wembley. They lost here, and Greece has yet to send another club to one.
