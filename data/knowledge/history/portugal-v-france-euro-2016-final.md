## The short answer

The UEFA Euro 2016 final was played at Stade de France in Saint-Denis, France, on 10 July 2016. Portugal beat hosts France 1-0 after extra time. Referee: Mark Clattenburg (England). Attendance: 75,868. Cristiano Ronaldo was named man of the match.

## The road to the final

Portugal reached their first European Championship final after a remarkable tournament: they drew all three group matches, then won three knockout ties, two of them in extra time or on penalties. France, as hosts, were the favourites and had beaten Germany in the semi-final.

## How the match unfolded

The match remained goalless through ninety minutes. Cristiano Ronaldo was carried off injured in the 25th minute after a challenge. In the 109th minute, substitute Éder scored the only goal with a low shot from distance to give Portugal a 1-0 lead, which they held to the final whistle.

## What the final meant

- Portugal's first major international title, after decades of near-misses.
- A first European Championship crown, making them the tenth nation to win the tournament.
- Éder's late strike became one of the most celebrated goals in Portuguese football history.
- Ronaldo, despite leaving injured, was named man of the match for his earlier influence.

## Match details

The final at Stade de France on 10 July 2016 was refereed by England's Mark Clattenburg before 75,868 spectators. Portugal had drawn all three group games and needed extra time or penalties in three knockout matches; in the final they again went the distance. Ronaldo, their captain, was carried off injured in the 25th minute, but the match stayed goalless until the 109th minute, when Éder, a substitute, struck a low shot from outside the box past France goalkeeper Hugo Lloris. It was the only goal of the game. Portugal held on to win their first major trophy, and Ronaldo was named man of the match. The victory ended a long sequence of near-misses for the Portuguese, who had previously lost a Euro final in 2004 and a World Cup semi-final in 2006.
