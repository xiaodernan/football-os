## The short answer

The 1964 European Nations' Cup final was played at the Santiago Bernabéu in Madrid on 21 June 1964. Spain beat the defending champions, the Soviet Union, 2-1 for their first European title. Referee: Arthur Holland (England). Attendance: 79,115. Chus Pereda and Marcelino scored the Spanish goals.

## The road to the final

Spain, managed by José Villalonga and captained by Ferran Olivella, came through two-legged ties against Romania, Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland, then beat Hungary 2-1 after extra time in the semi-final in Madrid. The Soviet Union, under Konstantin Beskov and captained by Valentin Ivanov, had a bye in the preliminary round, then eliminated Italy, Sweden and Denmark. The two sides had never met before; Spain had withdrawn from the 1960 tournament rather than play the Soviet Union.

## How the match unfolded

In the sixth minute Luis Suárez exchanged passes with Carlos Lapetra and crossed for Chus Pereda to head Spain into the lead. Galimzyan Khusainov equalised two minutes later after a weak shot was mishandled by goalkeeper José Ángel Iribar. The rest of the first half was a midfield struggle. With six minutes left, Feliciano Rivilla found Pereda, whose cross was not cleared by Viktor Shustikov; Marcelino headed in at the near post for the winner.

## What the final meant

- Spain's first European Championship, won on home soil in the second edition of the competition.
- The Soviet Union's second successive final; they had won the first one in 1960.
- Six Spanish players and two Soviet players made UEFA's team of the tournament.
- Beskov was dismissed on his return to Moscow after the broadcast of Franco celebrating the win angered Nikita Khrushchev.
