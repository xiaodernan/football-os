## The short answer

The 1988–89 European Cup final was played at the Camp Nou, Barcelona, on 24 May 1989 at 20:15 CEST — before 97,000 witnesses. Steaua București 0–4 Milan. Goals: Ruud Gullit 18', 38'; Marco Van Basten 28', 46'. Referee Karl-Heinz Tritschler (West Germany).

This was Milan's third European Cup — their first in 20 years (1969) — and the start of Arrigo Sacchi's golden age with the Rossoneri.

## What the route told us

Milan: First round: Vitosha (Bulgaria) 7–2 (2–0 away, 5–2 home); Second round: Red Star Belgrade 2–2 (1–1 away, 1–1 home — Milan won 4–2 on penalties); Quarter-finals: Werder Bremen 1–0 (0–0 away, 1–0 home); Semi-finals: Real Madrid 6–1 (1–1 away, 5–0 home).

Steaua București: First round: Sparta Prague 7–3 (5–1 away, 2–2 home); Second round: Spartak Moscow 5–1 (3–0 home, 2–1 away); Quarter-finals: IFK Göteborg 5–2 (0–1 away, 5–1 home); Semi-finals: Galatasaray 5–1 (4–0 home, 1–1 away).

## How the match decided itself

Sacchi's Milan were a different beast from the cotton-top 1980s side — covered in pure geometry. Gullit opened the score in the 18th minute. Van Basten added the second. Gullit made it 3–0 before half-time. Half time Steaua 0–3 Milan. Right at the beginning of the second half, Van Basten added the fourth. It was scene for Milan's first European Cup in 20 years.

## What the final meant

- Milan returned to the elite of European club football after a long absence.
- They would repeat in 1990 and win again in 1994 — that was the first double-up in history.
- It was the arrival of the Sacchi-Milan dynasty: Maldini, Baresi, Donadoni, Rijkaard, Van Basten, Gullit, and Ancelotti.
