## The short answer

The 2018 FIFA World Cup final was played at Luzhniki Stadium in Moscow, Russia, on 15 July 2018 — before 78,011 witnesses. France 4–2 Croatia. Goals: Mario Mandžukić 18' (own goal, France lead), Ivan Perišić 28' (Croatia), Antoine Griezmann 38' pen (France), Paul Pogba 59' (France), Kylian Mbappé 65' (France), Mario Mandžukić 69' (Croatia). Referee Néstor Pitana (Argentina). Antoine Griezmann was named man of the match.

It was France's second FIFA World Cup title, twenty years after their first triumph in 1998. Croatia reached the World Cup final for the first time in their history.

## What the route told us

France (Group C winners): beat Australia 2–1 and Peru 1–0, drew 0–0 with Denmark. Knockout stage: Argentina 4–3 (round of 16), Uruguay 2–0 (quarter-final), Belgium 1–0 (semi-final).

Croatia (Group D winners): beat Nigeria 2–0, Argentina 3–0, and Iceland 2–1. Knockout stage: Denmark 1–1 after extra time, 3–2 on penalties (round of 16), Russia 2–2 after extra time, 4–3 on penalties (quarter-final), England 2–1 after extra time (semi-final).

## How the match decided itself

France took the lead in the 18th minute when a Griezmann free kick was diverted into his own net by Mandžukić. Perišić levelled with a low strike in the 28th minute, but France regained the advantage before half-time after a penalty was awarded following a VAR review for handball and converted by Griezmann. In the second half, Pogba extended the lead in the 59th minute and Mbappé added a fourth in the 65th. Mandžukić pulled one back for Croatia in the 69th minute after capitalising on an error by Hugo Lloris, but France saw out the 4–2 victory.

## What the final meant

- **France's second world title** — adding 2018 to their 1998 triumph.
- **Croatia's historic run** — a first-ever World Cup final appearance after three extra-time knockout matches.
- **A high-scoring final** — six goals made it the highest-scoring World Cup final since 1966.
- **A new generation's imprint** — Mbappé became the second teenager to score in a World Cup final, after Pelé in 1958.
