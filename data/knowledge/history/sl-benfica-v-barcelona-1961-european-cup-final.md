## The short answer

The 1960–61 European Cup final was played at the Wankdorf Stadium, Bern, Switzerland, on 31 May 1961 at 19:00 CEST — before 26,732 witnesses. SL Benfica 3–2 Barcelona. Goals: Sándor Kocsis 21', Zoltán Czibor 75' for Barcelona; José Águas 31', Antoni Ramallets 32' (own goal), Mário Coluna 55' for Benfica. Referee Gottfried Dienst (Switzerland).

It was the first European Cup final not to feature Real Madrid, who had lifted the trophy in each of the competition's first five editions — Barcelona had eliminated the champions in the first round. Benfica lifted their first European Cup; under Béla Guttmann they defended the title in 1962 Amsterdam — the second European Cup winner after Real Madrid capable of back-to-back titles, and the first Portuguese club in a final.

## What the route told us

Barcelona (as La Liga runners-up, Real Madrid being champions): Preliminary round: Lierse 5–0 agg (2–0 H, 3–0 A); First round: Real Madrid 4–3 agg (2–2 A at the Bernabéu, 2–1 H at the Camp Nou) — the most storied tie of the era; Quarter-finals: Spartak Hradec Králové 5–1 (4–0 H, 1–1 A); Semi-finals: Hamburger SV 2–2 agg (1–0 H, 1–2 A) — a replay at a neutral venue was needed (away goals did not exist yet): Brussels, 3 May 1961, 1–0 (Evaristo) at the King Baudouin Stadium.

Benfica: Preliminary round: Heart of Midlothian 5–1 (2–1 A, 3–0 H); First round: Újpesti Dózsa 7–4 agg (6–2 H, 1–2 A); Quarter-finals: AGF Aarhus 7–2 (3–1 H, 4–1 A); Semi-finals: Rapid Wien 4–1 agg (3–0 H, 1–1 A). Their opponent was Austrian champions Rapid Wien — Benfica's first final.

## How the match decided itself

Barcelona were the favourites. Kocsis gave them the lead in the 21st minute. Águas equalised for Benfica in the 31st minute. A minute later, a Benfica defensive header drifted back toward goalkeeper Ramallets, who pushed the ball against his own crossbar — and the ball appeared to cross the line; referee Dienst awarded an own goal to Benfica (32').

Mario Coluna had broken his nose in the eighth minute; when Cavém put a cross into the box on the 55th, the winger cleared the ball directly back to Coluna, standing outside the penalty area, and Coluna volleyed it home for Benfica's third. Czibor pulled one back for Barcelona in the 75th minute, but Benfica held on.

## What the final meant

This was the end of Real Madrid's five-year dynasty — the first final not involving the five-time champions, and the first of Benfica's two consecutive European titles (they would repeat the trick in 1962 against Real Madrid at the Olympic Stadium in Amsterdam). Guttmann's Benfica were the first Portuguese club in a final, and Coluna's volley the hinge of the game. Antoni Ramallets of Barcelona never won the European Cup.
