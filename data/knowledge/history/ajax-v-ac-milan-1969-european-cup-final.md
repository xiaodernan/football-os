## The short answer

The 1968–69 European Cup final was played at the Estadio Santiago Bernabéu, Madrid, on 28 May 1969 at 19:30, before 31,782 witnesses. Ajax 1–4 AC Milan. Goals: Pierino Prati 7', 39', 74'; Angelo Sormani 66' for Milan; Velibor Vasović 61' (pen.) for Ajax. Referee José María Ortiz de Mendíbil (Spain).

Ajax — the first Dutch club to reach a European Cup final — lost this final; Rinus Michels' side would return and win it in 1971, 1972 and 1973. Milan entered this as their 1963 champions-era club and lifted their second European Cup. Their one previous final was the 1958 Heysel final, where they had lost 3–2 to Real Madrid. Gianni Rivera — 1969 Ballon d'Or — captained Milan.

## How the match decided itself

Prati scored in the seventh and again in the 39th; Ajax were two goals down at half time. Vasović pulled one back from the penalty spot in the 61st — the score 3–1. Sormani added the third on 66', and Prati completed his hat-trick on 74'. Rocco's Milan won with the structure Rocco had perfected elsewhere — disciplined lines, sudden counter-surges — and they did it in a year when Rivera's contribution was impossible to ignore: he was the 1969 Ballon d'Or.

## What the final meant

Prati remains, as of 2025, the most recent player to score a hat-trick in a European Cup or Champions League final. Rinus Michels' Ajax arrived at a historic first for the Netherlands — but lost, and would return in 1971 to win under the new Total Football doctrine. Milan's next final was 1989.
