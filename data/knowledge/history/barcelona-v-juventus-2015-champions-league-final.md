## The short answer

The 2014–15 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Olympiastadion in Berlin, Germany on 6 June 2015 at 20:45 local time — 26 °C, partly cloudy — in front of 70,442 people. Barcelona beat Juventus 3–1 to lift their fifth European Cup. Having already claimed league and cup in that same season, they closed out their second treble (2009 was the first). Goals: Ivan Rakitić 4' (Barcelona's fastest goal in a Champions League final, fourth-fastest in the competition's history; the goal capped a build-up on the left flank), Álvaro Morata 55' (equaliser; rebound from Carlos Tevez's hit at ter Stegen), Luis Suárez 68' (rebound — Buffon saved Messi's edge-of-the-area effort, Suárez followed in), Neymar 94' (the last kick of the match; Pedro's assist from a counter-attack). Referee Cüneyt Çakır (Turkey); Man of the match Andrés Iniesta; attendance 70,442. Xavi Hernández was brought on for Iniesta in stoppage time — his 767th and last Barcelona appearance.

## What the route told us

Juventus came through Group A as runners-up behind Atlético Madrid (10 points: losses at the Vicente Calderón and a 3–2 home win against Olympiacos). They then eliminated Borussia Dortmund 5–1 on aggregate (2–1 at home; 3–0 away), Monaco 1–0 on aggregate (Vidal's first-half penalty in Turin; 0–0 at Stade Louis II), and Real Madrid 3–2 on aggregate in the semi-final (2–1 at home; 1–1 at the Bernabéu). Barcelona came through Group F winners on 15 points, unbeaten; they eliminated Manchester City 3–1 on aggregate (2–1 at the Etihad; 1–0 at the Camp Nou), Paris Saint-Germain 5–1 on aggregate (3–1 at the Parc des Princes; 2–0 at the Camp Nou), Bayern Munich 5–3 on aggregate (3–0 at the Camp Nou; 2–3 at the Allianz).

## What the final meant

For Barcelona this was their second treble title of the Messi era (2009 and 2015), their fifth Champions League title, and the first Champions League final hosted in Berlin — a venue that had already been a World Cup final venue in 2006.

For Juventus, this was a sixth European Cup final defeat — the most by any single club in the competition's history (previously shared with Bayern Munich and Benfica).

For the tournament, this was the first Champions League final at the Olympiastadion in Berlin, a venue already used for a World Cup final in 2006.

And for Barcelona, MSN marked their arrival in Europe with a second continental treble — Messi, Suárez and Neymar producing the decisive strike on the night.
