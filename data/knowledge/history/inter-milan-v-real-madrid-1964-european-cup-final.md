## The short answer

The 1963–64 European Cup final was played at the Praterstadion, Vienna, Austria, on 27 May 1964 at 19:30 CET — before 71,333 witnesses. Inter Milan 3–1 Real Madrid. Goals: Sandro Mazzola 43', 76'; Aurelio Milani 61' for Inter; Felo 70' for Madrid. Referee Josef Stoll (Austria).

This was Inter Milan's first European Cup — they had never qualified for the competition before this edition. Helenio Herrera, architect of "Il Grande Inter", was the second coach after Béla Guttmann (1961/62) to manage consecutive winning finals.

## What the route told us

Inter Milan (Serie A champions): Preliminary round: Everton 1–0 agg (0–0 A, 1–0 H); First round: AS Monaco 4–1 agg (1–0 H, 3–1 A); Quarter-finals: Partizan 4–1 agg (2–0 A, 2–1 H); Semi-finals: Borussia Dortmund 4–2 agg (2–2 A in Dortmund, 2–0 H).

Real Madrid (La Liga champions): Preliminary round: Rangers 7–0 agg (1–0 A, 6–0 H); First round: Dinamo București 8–4 agg (3–1 A, 5–3 H); Quarter-finals: defending champions AC Milan 4–3 agg (4–1 H, 0–2 A at the San Siro); Semi-finals: Zürich 8–1 agg (2–1 A, 6–0 H).

## How the match decided itself

Mazzola put Inter ahead on 43 minutes. Milani doubled their lead in the 61st minute. Felo pulled one back for Real on 70 minutes, but 4 minutes later Mazzola completed his brace (76'), and Inter went on to win 3–1. It was Inter's first trophy in the European Cup and the first of their back-to-back titles under Herrera.

## What the final meant

This was the start of the "Great Inter" era — Herrera's catenaccio built around Sarti, Facchetti, Picchi, Jair, Suárez, Corso and Sandro Mazzola. Inter retained the trophy the following year — the 1965 final at the San Siro, again against Benfica, won 1–0. Herrera later took Inter to another final (1967), lost 2–1 to Celtic. Real Madrid went without a European Cup between 1962 and 1966.
