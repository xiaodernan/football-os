## The short answer

The UEFA Euro 2004 final was played at Estádio da Luz in Lisbon, Portugal, on 4 July 2004. Greece beat hosts Portugal 1-0. Referee: Markus Merk (Germany). Attendance: 62,865. Theodoros Zagorakis was named man of the match.

## The road to the final

Greece and Portugal were drawn together in Group A and met in the opening game, where Greece won 2-31 in what the BBC called a "shock defeat" for the hosts. Portugal won their other two group games against Russia and Spain; Greece drew with Spain and lost to Russia, finishing second. In the knockout stage Portugal beat England on penalties and then the Netherlands. Greece beat defending champions France in the quarter-finals and the Czech Republic in the semi-finals via a silver goal.

## How the match unfolded

The match stayed goalless until the 57th minute, when Angelos Basinas' corner was met by Angelos Charisteas, whose powerful header beat goalkeeper Ricardo. Greece held on to complete a 1-0 victory, completing one of the greatest upsets in tournament history.

## What the final meant

- Greece's first major title, in only their second ever European Championship appearance.
- Widely regarded as the greatest upset in European Championship history; Greece had never previously won a match at a major tournament and went in at 150-1.
- The only time in Euro history (as of 2026) that the same two nations played each other in both the opening match and the final of one tournament.
- Greece qualified for the 2005 FIFA Confederations Cup, though they later failed to qualify for the 2006 World Cup.

## Match details

The final at Estádio da Luz on 4 July 2004 was refereed by Germany's Markus Merk before 62,865 spectators. It was a rematch of the tournament's opening game, which Greece had won 2-31. The match was tense and goalless until the 57th minute, when Basinas' corner found Charisteas, whose powerful header beat Ricardo. Portugal, managed by Luiz Felipe Scolari, could not find an equaliser; Scolari later said Greece were the better team and lamented that Portugal had been beaten by "one piece of sloppy defending". Greece's manager Otto Rehhagel called it "an unusual achievement for Greek football and especially for European football". Zagorakis, the Greek captain, was named man of the match.
