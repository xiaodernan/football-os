## The short answer

The 1955–56 European Cup final was played at the Parc des Princes, Paris, on 13 June 1956 at 20:30 CET — before 38,239 witnesses. Real Madrid 4–3 Stade de Reims. Goals: Reims — Michel Leblond 6'; Jean Templin 10'; Michel Hidalgo 62'. Real Madrid — Alfredo Di Stéfano 14'; Héctor Rial 30', 79'; Marquitos 67'. Referee Arthur Edward Ellis (England).

This was Europe's first European Cup final — Madrid went on to win the competition five consecutive years, an feat of dominance that would not be repeated until 1970s Bayern.

## How the match decided itself

The match opened brightly for Reims, who were 2–0 up inside ten minutes — Leblond scored first in the 6th minute, then Templin doubled the lead in the 10th. Real Madrid rallied with a Di Stéfano strike (14') then a second by Rial (30') to level the game. Hidalgo restored Reims' lead in the 62nd minute. But Real Madrid answered — Marquitos (67') and Rial (79') stole the show — and they won 4–3 after holding out the final eleven minutes.

## What the final meant

- The European Cup's first final was Europe football's new headline; UEFA was founded two years later (1958).
- Real Madrid would go on to win the competition five consecutive years (1956–1960) — an untouched European mark.
- Four goals came in the first half, three after the hour mark.
