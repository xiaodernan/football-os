## The short answer

The 2009 FIFA Confederations Cup final was played at Ellis Park Stadium in Johannesburg, South Africa, on 28 June 2009. Brazil beat the United States 3-2. Referee: Martin Hansson (Sweden). Attendance: 52,291. Kaká was named man of the match.

## The road to the final

The USA and Brazil qualified as continental champions from 2007 and were drawn together in Group B with Egypt and Italy; Brazil won all three group games, including 3-0 over the USA. In the semi-finals the USA upset European champions Spain 2-0, while Brazil beat hosts South Africa 1-0.

## How the match unfolded

The United States, in their first major men's tournament final, stunned Brazil by taking a 2-0 lead before half-time. Brazil responded after the interval with three unanswered goals to win 3-2 — one of the great Confederations Cup comebacks.

## What the final meant

- Brazil's second consecutive Confederations Cup title.
- A remarkable comeback from two goals down in a tournament final.
- Kaká, central to the revival, was named man of the match.
- A 3.9 million US television audience, a record for a non-World Cup match at the time.

## Match details

The final at Ellis Park Stadium on 28 June 2009 was refereed by Sweden's Martin Hansson before 52,291 spectators. The United States, playing in the first major men's tournament final in their history, shocked Brazil with a 2-0 half-time lead. But the Brazilians, managed by Dunga, produced a second-half revival, scoring three times without reply to win 3-2. Kaká, whose influence drove the comeback, was named man of the match. The match drew 3.9 million television viewers in the United States — then a record for a non-World Cup fixture — and both finalists later reached the knockout stages of the 2010 World Cup.
