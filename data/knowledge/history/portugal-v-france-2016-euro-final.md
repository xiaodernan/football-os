## The short answer

The 2016 European Championship final was played at the Stade de France in Saint-Denis, France, on 10 July 2016. Portugal beat hosts France 1-0 after extra time. Referee: Mark Clattenburg (England). Attendance: 75,868. Pepe was named man of the match.

## The road to the final

Portugal finished third in Group F with three draws, then beat Croatia, Poland on penalties and Wales 2-0 in the semi-final. France won Group A, then beat the Republic of Ireland, Iceland and Germany 2-0 in the semi-final.

## How the match unfolded

A goalless ninety minutes saw Portugal captain Cristiano Ronaldo depart through injury. In the 108th minute substitute Eder struck a low shot from distance past the goalkeeper to give Portugal a 1-0 lead, which held to the final whistle and secured their first major tournament title.

## What the final meant

- Portugal's first major international trophy, twelve years after losing the 2004 final at home.
- The tenth different nation to win the European Championship.
- France became the second host to lose a Euro final, after Portugal in 2004.

## Match details

The final at the Stade de France in Saint-Denis on 10 July 2016 was refereed by Mark Clattenburg of England before 75,868 spectators. Portugal had drawn all three group games and needed extra time in two of their knockout ties, yet their disciplined approach carried them through. Eder's 108th-minute goal, a low shot from about twenty-five yards that beat goalkeeper Hugo Lloris, was the only goal of the game. It ended a twelve-year wait from the heartbreak of 2004 and gave Portugal their first major trophy, a triumph built on resilience rather than the injured Ronaldo.
