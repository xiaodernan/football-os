## The short answer

The 1996–97 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Olympiastadion in Munich on 28 May 1997 at 20:30 CEST — before 59,000 witnesses. Borussia Dortmund 3–1 Juventus. Goals: Karl-Heinz Riedle 29', 34', Lars Ricken 71' (Borussia Dortmund); Alessandro Del Piero 65' (Juventus). Referee Sándor Puhl (Hungary). Assistant referees László Hamar and Imre Bozóky; fourth official Attila Juhos.

It was Borussia Dortmund's first European Cup/Champions League title, and the third consecutive final in which the defending champions lost.

## What the route told us

Borussia Dortmund: Group B runners-up — Widzew Łódź 2–1 H and 2–2 A, Steaua București 3–0 A and 5–3 H, Atlético Madrid 1–0 A and 1–2 H. Quarter-finals Auxerre 4–1 agg (3–1 H, 1–0 A). Semi-finals Manchester United 2–0 agg (1–0 H, 1–0 A).

Juventus (defending champions): Group C winners — Manchester United 1–0 H and 1–0 A, Fenerbahçe 1–0 A and 2–0 H, Rapid Wien 1–1 A and 5–0 H. Quarter-finals Rosenborg 3–1 agg (1–1 A, 2–0 H). Semi-finals Ajax 6–2 agg (2–1 A, 4–1 H).

The match also reunited clubs that had met in the 1993 UEFA Cup final (won by Juventus over two legs) and in earlier European ties; several players had crossed between the sides, including Jürgen Kohler, Andreas Möller, Stefan Reuter and Paulo Sousa in Dortmund colours.

## How the match decided itself

Riedle struck twice in five minutes: a low finish from Paul Lambert's cross in the 29th minute, then a header from an Andreas Möller corner in the 34th. Dortmund controlled the first half and carried a 2–0 lead into the interval.

Juventus answered in the 65th minute when substitute Alessandro Del Piero scored with a back-heel from Alen Bokšić's cross. Six minutes later, Lars Ricken — on the pitch for only 16 seconds — ran onto Möller's through ball and chipped Angelo Peruzzi from distance with his first touch to restore Dortmund's two-goal cushion. 3–1.

## What the final meant

- **Dortmund's first European crown** — Ottmar Hitzfeld's side delivered Germany's first Champions League title since reunification-era club football's new cycle began.
- **Holders fall again** — for the third straight final, the defending champions were beaten (Milan 1995, Ajax 1996, Juventus 1997).
- **Lambert's containment, Zidane's frustration** — Paul Lambert's marking of Zinedine Zidane became one of the match's defining tactical stories.
- **Ricken's instant impact** — his goal, scored 16 seconds after coming on, remains one of the fastest by a substitute in a Champions League final.
