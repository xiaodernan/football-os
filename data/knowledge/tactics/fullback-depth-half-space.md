## The short answer

In the modern 4-3-3, the fullback's value is not the headline cross. It is the lane behind the winger: the **half-space** — the strip of grass between touchline and channel, especially on the strong side, where the fullback can step inside and receive between the opponent's winger and their holding midfielder. When that lane opens, the pass is the most dangerous ball the press of the 90 mins produces; the winger naturally drifts wide, the holding midfielder slides centrally to protect the penalty area; the fullback steps into the pocket between them and is free to turn.

## What makes the lane work

1. **Width with balance** — the team needs visible width to stretch the opponent, but a fullback committing forward without a counterpart striker or a holding midfielder behind is abandoning the mid-block. The 4-3-3 usually works because one of the three midfielders slides laterally to cover.
2. **The on-ball timing** — the fullback should not arrive at the pocket before the press releases. If the ball is on the far side, the nearest midfielder holds deeper and the fullback reads the pressing trigger. The verb is *delay*, not *sprint*.
3. **The mid-fielder's body shape** — if the central midfielder is already facing his own goal when the pass arrives, the fullback should not step into the pocket; the ball gets played back or lateral, and the forward progression resets.

## The failure pattern

The classic 4-3-3 failure is the fullback already arriving on the touchline with no support arriving behind. The winger freezes because delivery looks impossible into the box; the striker tucks in, leaving the right channel abandoned. The fullback has sprinted into nothing and the opponent's counterattacking winger is facing a half-built build-up. The fix is positional anticipation: the fullback reads whether the midfielder he commits past is capable of covering him, or runs on the blind side of the man with the ball.

## What the press should concede

A well-drilled 4-3-3 will happily concede the back pass to reset the attack; it does not panic because the fullback is already on the touchline. This calm reset is the difference between a stretched build-up and an organized one; it is the visible behaviour of a system the pressing player must not allow to restart.
