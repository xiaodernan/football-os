## The short answer

When a winger holds the ball on the touchline, the press must close the lane to the half-space carrier and the diagonal inside pass before the wide player completes the turn on the outside touch. The trap is set with one wide forward on the touchline's outside shoulder and one inside midfielder sitting deep enough to pounce the inside lane. The winger on the ball is then forced backward into a pass or into contention with the outside shoulder. Most modern 4-3-3 systems that want to destroy wide possession set this trap off the second pressing trigger, not the first.

## What the trap is, exactly

The wide forward pins the touchline; the inside midfielder holds a covering position on the half-space. The midfielder must do two things without stepping out too early:

1. Predict where the winger's first touch is going to land — because the touch deep down the wing is the entire trigger to step up, not the player receiving the ball.
2. Be close enough to pounce the square inside pass without stepping out of the zone — but not so close that the ball carrier can just pass it.

Once the winger turns on the outside touch, the touchline becomes the boundary and the trigger becomes live. The fullback of the pressing team has to double-team the winger; the inside midfielder cheats toward the half-space to cut the inside lane; the central midfielder must mark the dangerous half-space carrier with a better angle than any other pressing player.

## Why the trap is fragile

- It only works if the winger has no time to look up. If the build-up outpaces the press, the trap never fires and the fullback must chase, which abandons the back line.
- The inside midfielder must rotate without leaving his man in the half-space uncovered.

## What the press should concede

If the winger tees up a clean lateral pass to the fullback behind the wide forward, the press should not commit. The wide forward should stay on the touchline, the inside midfielder should hold zone, and the centre-back must have already read the route of the cross. The winger who tees up a clean lateral is still cosseted, but the press gets paid for patience; it does not chase.
