## The short answer

The ball that is actually contested is the second ball — the one that drops after a duel, a clearance, a save, a loose touch from a build-up pass. The first ball is a contest; the second ball is the *territory*. Win the second, then you win the entire duel chain that follows it.

In the modern game the press lives here. The pressing trigger is not a pass; it is a dead ball — a half-cleared argument on half-won ground where attention drifts to the next contest and the second ball is won by whoever refuses to look away. This is the whole brief: the second ball is the ball you decide to win, on purpose.

## Why the second ball matters

1. **Spatial drifts** — the first ball is contested wherever it sat; the second ball sits one touch later, on the diagonal of where attention has moved. That one touch turns a neutral into a live duel.
2. **Cognitive bandwidth** — after the first contest the loser has already started his next task; the winner hasn't.
3. **Width-by-default** — the second ball is always wider than the first; winning it stretches the defense across the turnover.

## The cue for the press

A good press steps in on the second ball with a body shape that hides the next goal. The shape matters more than the aggression. The pressing body runs to cover the lane the ball would land on, not the ball itself; once the touch and drop are committed, the lane is the live target.

## What the press should concede — and what it shouldn't

The press should concede nothing second-balled while the ball is loose in the half-space; it should be ready to chase after one touch when the ball lands cleanly. The worst concession is chasing a pass after the press has already made the first step towards the wrong target. The press wins for the next ball, not the ball on the floor at the moment it bounces.
