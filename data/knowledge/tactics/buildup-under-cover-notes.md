## The short answer

Against a mid-block, the 4-3-3 does not try to break the first line on one pass. It manipulates three variables — the depth of the first line, the available diagonal foot passes between the high fullback and the holding midfielder, and the natural positional gap where the opponent's wide forwards drift away from the ball. The cycle goes:

1. Centre-backs split wide and hold the line at 16-22 metres from goal.
2. Holding midfielder drops between the CB channels, opens the diagonal pass angle.
3. Fullback advances to the touchline, holds position before the winger enters.
4. Winger attacks the half-space or touchline, drawing the opponent's wide midfielder up.
5. The fullback's pace is used to overwork the opposition's opposite flank — not to sprint, but to offset a defensive resource elsewhere.

## When the system works

It works against a mid-block that wants to keep two CB channels closed but cannot afford to leave the CB-to-fullback diagonal open. The holding midfielder must take the first step under the press (not behind it), and the centre-back must split wide enough that a pass to the fullback is reachable.

## Where the system breaks down

- If the fullback commits forward while the central midfielder is in transition rather than in position, the mid-block can pivot and press the line of the pass.
- If the winger refuses to attack the half-space and instead sits on the touchline, the fullback's overlapping run loses all value.
- If the holding midfielder allows the forward line to carry him out of the block, the system resets unsafely.
