## The short answer

The eight-second window after your team loses the ball is the cheapest possession in football. The opposition has not organised itself yet, the ball is in a chaotic area in front of the man who just lost it, and every pass option they want is guarded by your own closing players. Win the ball back inside that window and the game re-starts from the same field position without a single attacking phase thrown away. Let the window close and the transition becomes their counterattack instead of your counter-press.

## Why the window is eight seconds

Eight seconds is not a magic number — it is the realistic budget for the losing side to (a) see the ball is not in play for their purposes, (b) orientate, and (c) pick one of the three outlets they always want. In the first two seconds the losing side is looking at the referee and the ball. Between two and five seconds the pivot and the winger are picking their angles. By eight seconds the shape has settled, the ball has a receiver, and the press is now an organised defensive shape playing against an organised attack. The counter-press only exists inside the chaos.

## What makes a counter-press

1. **The man who lost it** — every counter-press starts with the player who lost possession. He is already turned towards the ball, the only useful fact in the first second.
2. **The nearest two** — the two closest teammates shut the immediate pass outlets. Not chasing the ball; closing passing lanes.
3. **The safety man** — one player stays deep enough to stop the long clearance. Everyone else floods forward.

## Where the counter-press breaks

- If the player who lost the ball retreats into his own half rather than closing it, the window is gone before it started.
- If all three press the ball carrier but the nearest outlet is unmarked, the out-ball plays through the press and the counter-attack starts on your flank.
- If a cheap yellow card kills the first counter-press of the half, the second will not be pressed — the rhythm of the window matters.

## When not to counter-press

Counter-press only against a side whose first outlets you have specifically identified. Against a direct side whose first instinct is "punt to the 9 and run", the eight-second window is a trap — they have practised exactly that launch. Against such a side, drop and make them play.
